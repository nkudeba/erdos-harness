## Answer

**1. Recognition**

Yes. This is the **Crittenden–Vanden Eynden theorem**: if n arithmetic progressions cover the integers 1, …, 2ⁿ, they cover all of ℤ. Erdős conjectured it.

- R. B. Crittenden and C. L. Vanden Eynden announced it in Bull. AMS (1969), "Any n arithmetic progressions covering the first 2ⁿ integers cover all integers".
- The full paper appeared in Proc. AMS (1970).
- The bound 2ᵐ is sharp: the progressions 2ʲ⁻¹ mod 2ʲ cover 1..2ᵐ−1 but miss 0.
- I believe it is catalogued on erdosproblems.com, but I don't recall the problem number.
- Later short proofs and generalisations exist, including work by Z.-W. Sun and a short proof by Balister–Bollobás–Morris–Sahasrabudhe–Tiba (c. 2020).

Confidence that this is the theorem: high (~90%). Confidence in the exact citation details: moderate.

The Lean rendering matches the statement. The m = 0 case is vacuous, because `Fin 0` is empty and so `hblock` is false.

**2. Strategy I would try first**

Argue by contradiction: suppose some z is uncovered.

1. Take ω_j = e^{2πi/q_j} and define g(x) = ∏_j (1 − ω_j^{x − u_j}) on ℤ. Then g(x) = 0 exactly when x lies in some class, so g(z) ≠ 0.
2. Expanding the product writes g as a sum of at most 2ᵐ exponentials c_S·e(α_S x). After merging equal frequencies, there are N ≤ 2ᵐ distinct frequencies. Not all coefficients are zero, since g(z) ≠ 0.
3. An exponential sum with N distinct frequencies in [0,1) cannot vanish at N consecutive integers. The system is a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct values e(α_S), which is nonsingular.
4. So g is nonzero somewhere in t, …, t + 2ᵐ − 1. That contradicts `hblock`.

This is essentially the known exponential-sum / Vandermonde argument. I believe I *remember* it from the literature, in the style of the short modern proofs, rather than inventing it now. I'm not certain which paper presents it in exactly this form.

For Lean, a variant may be easier. It avoids complex roots of unity by working with the indicator functions of the classes, which are periodic. It then uses the fact that a product of m functions of the form (1 − periodic indicator) satisfies a linear recurrence of order ≤ 2ᵐ.
