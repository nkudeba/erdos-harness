## Answer

**1. Recognition**

Yes. This is Erdős Problem #350 on erdosproblems.com, which is where the problem is catalogued. I believe it is listed as solved, with the proof attributed to Ryavec and reported by Erdős. The sharp form I recall is $\sum_{a\in A} 1/a \le 2 - 2^{1-|A|}$, with equality for $A=\{1,2,4,\dots,2^{n-1}\}$. I am confident about the problem number and that it is a known solved result. I am less sure of the exact attribution and the original publication details.

**2. Proof strategy**

Write $A=\{a_1<\dots<a_n\}$ and set $b_i=2^{i-1}$.

- **Partial-sum bound.** The $2^k$ subsets of $\{a_1,\dots,a_k\}$ have distinct sums. Those sums are nonnegative integers, all at most $a_1+\dots+a_k$. Hence $\sum_{i\le k} a_i \ge 2^k-1 = \sum_{i\le k} b_i$ for every $k$.
- **Abel summation.** Write $1/b_i - 1/a_i = (a_i-b_i)\,w_i$ with $w_i = 1/(a_i b_i)$. Since both sequences increase, $w_i$ is positive and decreasing.
- **Conclusion.** Let $D_k = \sum_{i\le k}(a_i-b_i)$, which is $\ge 0$ by the first step. Abel summation gives $\sum_i (a_i-b_i)w_i = \sum_{k<n} D_k(w_k-w_{k+1}) + D_n w_n \ge 0$. Therefore $\sum_i 1/a_i \le \sum_i 2^{1-i} = 2-2^{1-n} < 2$.
- **Lean-specific point.** $0\notin A$, because $\{0\}$ and $\emptyset$ would have the same sum. So the convention $1/0=0$ in Lean never matters.

The partial-sum counting step is a standard argument I remember from the distinct-subset-sums literature. The Abel-summation completion is the argument I reconstructed just now. I believe it matches the known proof, but I can't point to a specific source for it.
