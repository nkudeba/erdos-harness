**1. Recognition.**
Yes, I recognise the mathematical content, though not under the given name. `erdos_61249` looks like a synthetic or masked id, and I do not associate this statement with a particular Erdős problem number.

The statement is a standard consequence of the van der Waerden permanent bound. For a doubly stochastic N×N matrix, perm(b) ≥ N!/N^N. This was conjectured by van der Waerden in 1926 and proved by Egorychev and, independently, Falikman in 1980–81. The permanent is a sum of N! products ∏ b(i,τ(i)), so the largest product is at least N^(−N).

The weaker statement itself, that some permutation product is ≥ N^(−N), also appears as a known elementary lemma or exercise. I associate it with the Marcus–Minc literature, but I am not confident of the exact attribution. I am about 90% confident about the connection to van der Waerden and about 40% confident about any specific citation for the elementary version.

**2. Strategy.**
I would avoid the full van der Waerden theorem and use Birkhoff–von Neumann plus an entropy/Jensen averaging argument. The steps are:

- Write b = Σ_k θ_k P_{σ_k}, a convex combination of permutation matrices with θ_k > 0. Mathlib has Birkhoff's theorem in `Mathlib.Analysis.Convex.DoublyStochasticMatrix`, though I would need to `#check` the exact name.
- Every σ_k in the support satisfies b(i,σ_k(i)) ≥ θ_k > 0, so the logarithms below are finite.
- Average: Σ_k θ_k Σ_i log b(i,σ_k(i)) = Σ_{i,j} b_ij log b_ij.
- Each row is a probability vector on N points, so its entropy is at most log N. That gives Σ_j b_ij log b_ij ≥ −log N for each row, so the total is ≥ −N log N.
- Some σ_k therefore has Σ_i log b(i,σ_k(i)) ≥ −N log N, i.e. ∏_i b(i,σ_k(i)) ≥ N^(−N).
