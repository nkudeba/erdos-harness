## Answer

**1. Recognition.** Yes. This is Erdős problem #499 on erdosproblems.com. It is closely tied to the van der Waerden permanent conjecture, which says that per(M) ≥ n!/nⁿ for doubly stochastic M. That conjecture was proved independently by Egorychev and by Falikman around 1980–81. Given that bound, the average of ∏ᵢ a_{iσ(i)} over the n! permutations is at least n⁻ⁿ, so some σ attains it. The answer is therefore yes.

**2. Strategy I would try first.**
- Use Birkhoff–von Neumann to write M = Σₖ λₖ P_{σₖ}, with λₖ > 0 summing to 1. Every σₖ is supported inside supp(M), so every logarithm below is finite.
- Compute the weighted average Σₖ λₖ log ∏ᵢ a_{iσₖ(i)}. Regrouping by entries gives Σ_{i,j} a_{ij} log a_{ij}.
- Each row is a probability vector, so its entropy is at most log n. Hence each row contributes Σⱼ a_{ij} log a_{ij} ≥ −log n, and the total is at least −n log n.
- Some σₖ must be at least the weighted average, so ∏ᵢ a_{iσₖ(i)} ≥ n⁻ⁿ.
