## Answer

**1. Recognition.** Yes. This is the **Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture** for restricted sumsets: |B ∔ B| ≥ min(p, 2|B| − 3) in ℤ/pℤ. The id "94163" does not match anything I know, so I take it to be a synthetic or masked label, but the statement itself is unmistakable. I am highly confident (>95%) in this identification.

- Erdős and Heilbronn posed it in the 1960s. It is stated in Erdős–Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980).
- It was first proved by **J. A. Dias da Silva and Y. O. Hamidoune** (Bull. London Math. Soc., 1994), using exterior algebra and the representation theory of the symmetric group.
- A second, much shorter proof is by **N. Alon, M. B. Nathanson and I. Z. Ruzsa** (Amer. Math. Monthly 1995; J. Number Theory 1996). It uses the polynomial method, later packaged as the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz.

The small edge cases hold under the ℤ-valued `min`:
- |B| ≤ 1 gives a negative bound, so the claim is trivial.
- ℓ = 2 with |B| = 2 gives a bound of 1, and the sumset {1} has one element.

**2. Strategy.** I would follow the Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa argument, which I **remember** from the literature rather than propose fresh.

- If 2|B| − 3 ≥ ℓ, first shrink B to a subset of size k with 2k − 3 ≤ ℓ (the case k = (ℓ+3)/2 needs care), so assume 2k − 3 < ℓ.
- Suppose the restricted sumset lies in a set C with |C| = 2k − 4. Consider the polynomial f(x,y) = (x − y) · ∏_{c∈C} (x + y − c). It has degree 2k − 3 and vanishes on all of B × B.
- The coefficient of x^{k−1} y^{k−2} is C(2k−4, k−2) − C(2k−4, k−1) = (2k−4)!/((k−1)!(k−2)!) · (1/(k−2)·… ), in fact a ballot-type number. It is nonzero mod ℓ because 2k − 4 < ℓ.
- The Combinatorial Nullstellensatz, applied with |B| = k > k − 1 and k > k − 2, then gives a point of B × B where f is nonzero. This is a contradiction.

In Lean, the main work is:
- the Nullstellensatz itself (Mathlib may now have a version under `MvPolynomial`; I would `#check` rather than assume a name);
- the coefficient extraction;
- the reduction to a subset of the right size.

**3. Specific proofs.** The published proofs are Dias da Silva–Hamidoune (1994) and Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa (1995/1996). Alon's "Combinatorial Nullstellensatz" survey (Combin. Probab. Comput., 1999) also reproduces the argument.

I do **not** reliably remember a specific Lean or Mathlib formalization of Erdős–Heilbronn. Mathlib does contain Cauchy–Davenport, which is the unrestricted analogue, but I would not rely on any particular name for a restricted-sumset result without checking.
