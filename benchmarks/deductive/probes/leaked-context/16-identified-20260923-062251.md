## Answer

1. **Recognition.** Yes. This is Erdős Problem #16 on erdosproblems.com. It asks about the odd integers not of the form 2^k + p. Romanoff showed that numbers of the form 2^k + p have positive lower density. Erdős, using covering systems, showed that there is an infinite arithmetic progression of odd numbers not of this form. He then asked whether the exceptional odd set is essentially just one such progression plus a density-zero set.

   I believe the answer is no. I attribute the negative answer to Yong-Gao Chen (around 2023), and the site marks the problem as solved/disproved. The Lean statement encodes exactly that negative answer.

   My confidence that this is #16 and that it was answered in the negative is fairly high, about 80–85%. I am less sure about the exact attribution and year, about 60%.

2. **Proof strategy.** Suppose U = {a + md} ∪ B with B of density zero. Equality forces two things: the whole progression lies inside U, and U minus the progression has density zero. I would refute the second.

   - Use several different covering systems of powers of 2. Each gives residue classes, modulo various M, of odd n with n − 2^k divisible by a small prime from the covering set for every k. Those n are non-representable, apart from finitely many cases where n − 2^k is itself that prime.
   - Pick such a class a' mod M' that meets the complement of a + dℕ in a set of positive density. That forces U \ (a + dℕ) to have positive upper density, so B cannot have density zero.
   - One also has to handle degenerate progressions. If d is even with a even, the progression contains even numbers not in U. If the progression contains a number 2^k + p, which Dirichlet's theorem, via the imported `PrimesInAP`, can supply when the residues allow, it is not inside U.
   - The crux is producing two "independent" covering constructions, so that no single progression can absorb both. For example, use two coverings whose moduli involve primes not dividing d. Alternatively, show the non-representable classes are not all contained in one residue class mod d.

   This is a strategy I am proposing now, informed by the general Erdős covering-system technique that I do remember. I do not remember the details of Chen's argument well enough to say it is his route.
