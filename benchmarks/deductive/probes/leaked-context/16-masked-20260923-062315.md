## Answer

**1. Recognition.** Yes. This is Erdős Problem #16 on erdosproblems.com. It asks whether the set of odd integers not of the form 2^k + p is the union of an infinite arithmetic progression and a set of density zero. Erdős raised it, in his covering-congruence papers and problem collections, after his 1950 covering-system construction.

I recall it was answered negatively by Yong-Gao Chen, in a paper of about 2023 titled something like "On the set of odd numbers not of the form 2^k + p". I'm fairly confident about the problem identification (around 85%). I'm less sure of the exact author, year and title (around 60%).

The theorem id 45592 appears to be synthetic or masked. This workspace's own notes also list #16 as a saved deductive-benchmark solve.

**2. Strategy.** I remember the broad shape from the literature, but the details below are my own reconstruction, not recalled.

- **Parity case.** If e is odd, or b is even with e even, the progression contains an even number. V contains only odd numbers, so the equality fails at once.
- **Main case (b and e both odd-compatible).** Suppose V = {b + m·e} ∪ C with C of density zero.
- **Covering systems.** Use covering systems for the powers of 2, in the style of Erdős's {0 mod 2, 0 mod 3, 1 mod 4, 3 mod 8, 7 mod 12, 23 mod 24} with primes 3, 5, 7, 13, 17, 241. Combine them via the Chinese remainder theorem to build a residue class r mod M with three properties:
  - it is odd;
  - it avoids the class b mod e (possibly after multiplying or twisting the moduli to be compatible with e);
  - every n in it, minus any 2^k, is divisible by one of the covering primes.
- **Positive-density subset.** That class, minus the finitely many exceptions where n − 2^k equals the covering prime itself, is a positive-density subset of V. It lies outside the progression, so it would have to sit inside C, which contradicts density zero.
- **Formalization pain points.** The hard parts in Lean are the explicit covering certificate (checkable by `decide` on small moduli), handling the exceptional cases, and a counting lemma for ncard of residue classes in Iic x.
