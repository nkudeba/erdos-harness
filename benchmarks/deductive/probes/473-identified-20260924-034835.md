**2. Strategy.** This is a strategy I am proposing now, though its outline may echo the known argument. I do not remember a source's details.

- Build the permutation greedily in stages. Keep a finite injective prefix whose last element is x.
- At each stage, let n be the smallest positive integer not yet used, and connect x to n through a few large, fresh "bridge" numbers.
- One bridge y would need x+y and y+n both prime infinitely often. That is a Hardy–Littlewood-type prime-pairs problem, which is open, so one bridge is not enough.
- With two bridges y₁ = q − x and y₂ = r − n, the conditions become: q prime, r prime, and q + r − (x+n) prime.
- That is a ternary linear equation in primes. Vinogradov/circle-method results, with parity handled by choosing y₁ and y₂ even, should give infinitely many large solutions. These avoid the finitely many used numbers and keep all terms positive and distinct.
- Every m is eventually reached as the least unused number, so the map is surjective.
