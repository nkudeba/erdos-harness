**1. Recognition**

Yes. This is Erdős Problem #499 on erdosproblems.com. The answer is affirmative, and I believe the site lists it as solved.

- **Main attribution:** Marcus and Minc, early 1960s (around 1962). I am fairly confident of this, but not certain of the exact citation.
- **Independent route:** it also follows from the van der Waerden permanent conjecture, proved by Egorychev and Falikman in 1981. That result gives perm(M) ≥ n!/nⁿ. The permanent is a sum of n! products, so some product is at least n⁻ⁿ.

I am highly confident the statement is true. I am moderately confident about who first proved it and when.

The Lean statement looks like a faithful rendering. It is probably taken from a "formal conjectures" style repository.

**2. Strategy (entropy + Birkhoff)**

I would use Birkhoff–von Neumann to write M = Σₖ λₖ P_{σₖ} as a convex combination of permutation matrices, with every λₖ > 0.

1. Every σₖ in the support has a_{iσₖ(i)} > 0 for all i, so log a_{iσₖ(i)} is defined.
2. Taking the λ-weighted average of Σᵢ log a_{iσₖ(i)} gives Σ_{i,j} a_ij log a_ij, using 0·log 0 = 0.
3. Each row is a probability vector on n points, so its entropy is at most log n. That is, Σⱼ a_ij log a_ij ≥ −log n.
4. The average is therefore at least −n log n. So some σₖ has Σᵢ log a_{iσₖ(i)} ≥ −n log n, which means ∏ᵢ a_{iσₖ(i)} ≥ n⁻ⁿ.

I think this is essentially the classical short proof, but I am partly reconstructing it now rather than recalling it verbatim from a source.

To formalize it, I would look for Mathlib's Birkhoff theorem, which I believe is `doublyStochastic_eq_convexHull_permMatrix` or something similar. I would also need Jensen/Gibbs for the entropy bound, for example via `Real.add_pow_le_pow_mul_pow_of_add_eq_one` or concavity of log. The case n = 1 is trivial.
