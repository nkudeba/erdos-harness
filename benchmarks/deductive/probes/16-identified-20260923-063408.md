## Answer

**1. Recognition**

Yes. This is Erdős Problem #16 on erdosproblems.com. It asks about odd integers not of the form $2^k+p$, the Romanoff–Erdős circle of problems. Erdős used covering congruences to show that such integers include a full arithmetic progression, for example $7629217 \pmod{11184810}$.

My recollection is that the site lists the problem as disproved (answer "no"), attributed to Yong-Gao Chen (around 2023). I believe he showed the set is not an infinite AP together with a density-zero set. The Lean statement asserts exactly that negation, for every $a$, $d>0$ and density-zero $B$, which matches.

- I am fairly confident (~75%) that the status is "disproved".
- I am less confident (~55%) that Chen is the author and about the exact year and title.

**2. Strategy I would try (proposed now, informed by the general covering-system method rather than a remembered proof)**

1. Suppose $U = \{a+md\} \cup B$. Then the progression $a+d\mathbb{N}$ lies inside $U$.
2. Use two explicit covering systems (Erdős-style) to get two progressions $r_1 \pmod{M_1}$ and $r_2 \pmod{M_2}$, both entirely inside $U$.
3. Any progression inside $U$ that is not contained in $a+d\mathbb{N}$ loses a positive-density subset to the complement. That subset would have to lie in $B$, which is impossible. So $d \mid M_1$, $d \mid M_2$, and $a \equiv r_1 \pmod d$, $a \equiv r_2 \pmod d$.
4. This forces $d$ to divide a fixed $g = \gcd(M_1, M_2)$. The last step is to show that no residue class modulo a divisor of $g$ lies entirely in $U$. For each odd class, exhibit an explicit element $2^k+p$, by direct computation or Dirichlet's theorem on primes in progressions, which Mathlib has in `PrimesInAP`.
5. The key choice is covering systems whose moduli share a small gcd.

The main formalization costs are:
- verifying the covering systems, which is decidable finite computation;
- proving the density contradiction via `ncard` bounds.

I cannot guarantee that suitable coverings exist with small enough gcd. That is precisely the content I attribute to Chen's work.
