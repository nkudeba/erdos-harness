## Answer

**1. Recognition.** Yes. This is Erdős problem #275, and it is a solved problem. I recognise it as the theorem of **Crittenden and Vanden Eynden**: any r arithmetic progressions that cover 2^r consecutive integers cover all integers. It appeared as "Any n arithmetic progressions covering the first 2^n integers cover all integers", Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., around 1970. There is an earlier announcement around 1969, and Erdős posed the question in the 1960s.

- I am highly confident (~90%) of the identification and the authors.
- I am less confident (~70%) of the exact year and title.
- I also recall that 2^r is sharp. The system a_i = 2^{i-1} mod 2^i, for i = 1..r, covers 1..2^r − 1 but misses 0 mod 2^r. I believe later, simpler proofs exist (e.g. by Balister et al., and related work of Z.-W. Sun), but I am not confident of those details.
