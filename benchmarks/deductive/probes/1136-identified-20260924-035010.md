**2. Strategy I would try (proposed now, not remembered).**
I would start from the multiples of 3 and try to add a positive-density set of extra elements without creating a sum equal to a power of 2.

The natural framework is dyadic blocks [2^m, 2^{m+1}). Within such a block, a sum a + b can only hit 2^{m+1} or 2^{m+2}. That makes the constraints local and "reflection-like": a + b = 2^j pairs a with 2^j − a.

Concretely, I would look for a periodic-in-scale pattern. That means residue classes modulo 3·2^r, chosen differently on different portions of each dyadic block. The goal is to certify both that no sum equals 2^k and that every counting function (A ∩ [0, N)) / N stays above 1/3 + ε for all large N.
