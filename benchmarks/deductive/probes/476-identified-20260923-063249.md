## Answer

**1. Recognition**

Yes. This is the **Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture** on restricted sumsets in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$.

- It was proved by **J. A. Dias da Silva and Y. O. Hamidoune** (Bull. London Math. Soc., 1994), using exterior algebra and representation theory.
- A second, simpler proof is due to **Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa** ("Adding distinct congruence classes modulo a prime", Amer. Math. Monthly 1995; and J. Number Theory 1996). It uses the polynomial method, later packaged as Alon's Combinatorial Nullstellensatz.
- It is catalogued as Erdős problem #476 and marked solved.

I am highly confident about the identification (~95%).

On the Lean statement itself: the degenerate cases are harmless. For $|A| \le 1$ the left side is negative, and the use of ℤ handles that. So the formalization looks faithful.

**2. Strategy (remembered, from Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa / the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz)**

1. **Reduce the size of A.** Pass to a subset of $A$ of size $k$ with $2k-3 \le p$. Take $k = (p+3)/2$ if $A$ is large, since shrinking $A$ only shrinks the restricted sumset.
2. **Assume the contrary.** Suppose $A \hat{+} A \subseteq C$ with $|C| = 2k-4$, padding $C$ if necessary.
3. **Build the polynomial.** Consider $f(x,y) = (x-y)\prod_{c\in C}(x+y-c)$, which has total degree $2k-3$ and vanishes on $A\times A$.
4. **Compute the key coefficient.** The coefficient of $x^{k-1}y^{k-2}$ is $\binom{2k-4}{k-2}-\binom{2k-4}{k-1}$. This equals $\frac{(2k-4)!}{(k-1)!\,(k-1)!}$ up to a factor that is nonzero mod $p$, and the whole quantity is nonzero mod $p$ because $2k-4 < p$.
5. **Apply the Nullstellensatz.** Use it with $S_1 = A$ (size $k > k-1$) and $S_2 = A$ minus one element (size $k-1 > k-2$). This gives a point where $f \ne 0$, a contradiction.

In Lean, the main costs would be the binomial-coefficient-nonvanishing computation, the case split on $2|A|-3$ versus $p$, and extracting the top coefficient of the product polynomial.

- **Published proofs:** Dias da Silva–Hamidoune (1994) and Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa (1995/96). The argument is also in Alon's "Combinatorial Nullstellensatz" (Combin. Probab. Comput. 1999) and in Tao–Vu, *Additive Combinatorics*.
