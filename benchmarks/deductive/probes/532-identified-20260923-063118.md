## Answer

1. **Recognition.** Yes, with high confidence. This is **Hindman's theorem**, also called the Finite Sums theorem. Neil Hindman proved it in 1974 ("Finite sums from sequences within cells of a partition of N", J. Combin. Theory Ser. A). It confirmed a conjecture of Graham and Rothschild, which Erdős also posed. erdosproblems.com lists #532 as solved (proved) by Hindman. The Lean statement looks like the formalization in the google-deepmind `formal-conjectures` repository, though I can't confirm that from memory.

2. **Strategy.** This is a remembered strategy, not a new one: the Galvin–Glazer ultrafilter proof.
   - The key tool is an idempotent ultrafilter `U` on the positive naturals (or `ℕ+`). It exists by the Ellis–Numakura lemma, since βℕ⁺ is a compact right-topological semigroup.
   - One colour class, say colour `i`, belongs to `U`.
   - Idempotence (`U + U = U`) lets you recursively pick `a₀ < a₁ < …` so that every finite sum stays inside a set in `U` contained in colour class `i`.
   - Take `A` to be the range of this strictly increasing sequence of positive integers.
   - Distinctness of elements makes `Finset` sums over subsets of `A` match the "FS of a sequence" notion.
   - Positivity comes from working on `ℕ+` or from intersecting with `{n | 0 < n}` in `U`.

   In Lean I would first try Mathlib's `Mathlib/Combinatorics/Hindman.lean`. It contains `Hindman.FS`, `Hindman.exists_idempotent_ultrafilter_le` and a partition-regularity result, which I believe is `Hindman.FS_partition_regular` (names to be verified by retrieval). The main extra work is translating from that stream formulation to this statement:
   - The stream formulation sums distinct *indices*, so I need a strictly increasing stream to get distinct values.
   - I then need to show that every nonempty finite subset of the range is a finite sum of that stream.

   Baumgartner's 1974 purely combinatorial proof is an alternative, but it is much longer to formalize.

3. **Known proofs.**
   - Published: Hindman (1974). The ultrafilter proof is due to Galvin and Glazer and appears in Hindman–Strauss, *Algebra in the Stone–Čech Compactification*. Baumgartner gave a short combinatorial proof (JCTA 1974).
   - Formal: Mathlib has Hindman's theorem in `Mathlib.Combinatorics.Hindman`, in stream form for semigroups and monoids. I don't specifically remember a Lean proof of this exact `Finset`/`Set` formulation.
