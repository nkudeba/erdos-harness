- **Construction.** Take n = m² − 1, so n + 1 = m² and n = (m − 1)(m + 1).
- **Condition for n + 1.** If m is composite, every prime p dividing m² satisfies p ≤ m/2. Then p² ≤ m²/4 < m².
- **Condition for n.** If m − 1 and m + 1 are both composite, every prime p dividing n satisfies p ≤ (m + 1)/2. Then p² ≤ (m + 1)²/4 < m² − 1 once m ≥ 3.
- **Infinitely many m.** Choose m ≡ 26 (mod 30), i.e. m = 30j + 26:

  - m − 1 = 30j + 25 is divisible by 5 and greater than 5, so composite.
  - m + 1 = 30j + 27 is divisible by 3 and greater than 3, so composite.
