## Answer

1. **Recognition.** Yes. This is the classical Erdős question on sets with distinct subset sums (a "dissociated" set, also called a "sum-distinct" set). Erdős asked for a bound on the sum of reciprocals of such a set. The sharp bound is ∑ 1/a_i ≤ 2 − 2^{1−n}, which is attained by the powers of 2. It is usually credited to Ryavec, with a proof reported in Erdős's survey papers and in the Erdős–Graham problem book (1980). Later short proofs are attributed to Hanson, Steele and Stenger, and I believe to P. E. Frenkel (1998). It probably appears on erdosproblems.com as a solved problem, but I do not remember its number. I am confident about the result itself, moderately confident about the Ryavec attribution, and unsure about the catalogue number.

2. **Proof strategy.** I would try the following, which I am partly remembering and partly reconstructing now.
   - First, 0 ∉ B, because otherwise ∅ and {0} would have equal sums.
   - Sort B as a₁ < … < a_n. The first k elements have 2^k distinct subset sums, all lying in [0, a₁+…+a_k]. So a₁+…+a_k ≥ 2^k − 1 = 1+2+…+2^{k−1} for every k.
   - These partial-sum inequalities say the sequence (a_i) weakly supermajorizes (2^{i−1}). Since x ↦ 1/x is convex and decreasing, the Tomić–Weyl / Karamata-type inequality, or a direct Abel-summation argument, gives ∑ 1/a_i ≤ ∑ 1/2^{i−1} = 2 − 2^{1−n} < 2.
   - For Lean, I would avoid majorization theory. Instead I would prove by induction the Abel-summation step ∑ 1/a_i ≤ ∑ 1/2^{i−1}, using the partial-sum bounds and the monotonicity of the a_i.
   - The pigeonhole step, counting 2^k distinct subset sums inside a range of length ∑a_i + 1, uses `Finset.card_le_card_of_injOn` on the powerset.

3. **Existing proofs.** I recall published proofs by Ryavec (via Erdős–Graham) and later short proofs such as Frenkel's. I do not remember any specific formal Lean or Mathlib proof of this statement.
