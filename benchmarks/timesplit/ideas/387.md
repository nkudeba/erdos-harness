# 387

Is there an absolute constant c > 0 such that every binomial coefficient C(n,k) with 1 ≤ k < n has a divisor in the interval (cn, n]?

**Answer:** false

**Sources:** erdosproblems.com/387 (cached page); forum thread /forum/thread/387: Tao's proposed reduction to a covering problem plus a sieve step (1 Nov 2025), Gerbicz's k = 15 construction under Dickson's conjecture (27 Jan 2026), and Pratt's and Naprienko's posts (May–Jul 2026); proof-claims page (empty); Bui–Naprienko–Pratt–Zaharescu, arXiv:2605.21221, v2 (abstract, §1 and the §3 outline). The v1 covering step assumed GRH; v2 is unconditional.

**Key idea:** Write C(n,k) = ∏_{i<k} (n−i)/g_i with g_i | n−i and ∏ g_i = k!. A Schinzel-style construction with the Chinese remainder theorem places n in an arithmetic progression mod M ≈ e^k where every g_i exceeds B = 1/ε and C(n,k) has no prime factor ≤ k. This is a restricted covering problem: choose residue classes modulo the primes and prime powers up to k so that every i is covered enough, with Kummer's no-carry condition ruling out some classes. Then every cofactor (n−i)/g_i is at most εn, and any two cofactors multiply to more than n. A sieve over n makes the cofactors almost primes, and exponential-sum control of the error terms shows that for most such n no product of their prime factors lands in (εn, n].

**Main steps:**
- A divisor in (εn, n] arises either obviously, when one cofactor (n−i)/g_i exceeds εn, or by combining prime factors taken from several cofactors.
- Covering problem, handled unconditionally in v2: take a_p = 0 (mod p) for most primes p ≤ k. Add a few special choices: an auxiliary prime Q ≈ exp(log K/log log K) with a_Q = 1, primes q_d dividing k − d + 1 for a small "deficient" set of d, and primes ≡ 1 (mod Q) near k for the last survivors. Everything must respect the Kummer constraint involving k mod p, and averaging over k ~ K shows that many k admit a solution.
- CRT gives n ≡ γ (mod M) with every cofactor at most εn and no prime ≤ 2k dividing C(n,k).
- Under the prime k-tuples conjecture, the linear forms (γ−i)/g_i + (M/g_i)·r would all be prime for infinitely many r, which finishes the proof; Gerbicz's explicit example has k = 15. Unconditionally, sieve n ~ x in the progression so that C(n,k) has no prime factor ≤ x^{ε_k}, and bound the number of such n that have a divisor d ∈ (n/B, n] by a union bound with upper-bound sieve weights.
- The error terms have moduli near x. They are controlled by bilinear estimates for short incomplete Kloosterman sums, with Weyl differencing.
- The result gives infinitely many C(n,k) with k ≍ (log log n)^{1/2} that have no divisor in (n·(log log log log n)/(log log log n), n], so no absolute c works. For k ≥ exp((log n)^{2/3+ε}), a divisor near n does always exist.

**A hit looks like:** the notes force every numerator factor n−i to share a factor larger than 1/ε with k!, by CRT over the primes ≤ k and respecting Kummer's carry condition so that no small prime survives in C(n,k), which makes all cofactors ≤ εn. They then argue unconditionally, by a sieve that makes the cofactors almost primes followed by counting divisors in (εn, n], that products of the cofactors' prime factors miss the interval. Tao's two-part reduction (a covering problem and a divisor problem), with an unconditional plan for both parts, counts as a hit.

**A partial hit looks like:** the same CRT or covering construction finished only under prime k-tuples, Dickson's conjecture or GRH; Schinzel's C(99215, 15) mechanism without letting ε → 0; the positive result for large k; or numerics such as the explicit k = 15 examples.

**Confidence:** high. The notes come from the authors' own proof outline in arXiv:2605.21221, which matches Tao's forum reduction.
