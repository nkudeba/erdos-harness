# 884

For the divisors d₁ < ⋯ < d_t of n, is Σ_{i<j} 1/(d_j − d_i) ≪ 1 + Σ_i 1/(d_{i+1} − d_i) with an absolute implied constant?

**Answer:** false

**Sources:** erdosproblems.com/884 (cached page); forum thread /forum/thread/884: Tao's conditional disproof (10 Sep 2025), Larsen's unconditional disproof with Tao's endorsement (29 Mar 2026), and honicky's Lean formalisation (Jul 2026); proof-claims page (empty); Tao, "On the sum of reciprocals of gaps between divisors" (note linked from the forum, terrytao.wordpress.com, Sep 2025); Larsen, "A question of Erdős on reciprocals of gaps between divisors" (github.com/Larsen-Daniel/Erdos-884, 884.pdf).

**Key idea:** Write T for the all-pairs sum and S for the consecutive-gap sum over the divisors: K points in an interval of length H have Σ_{i<j} 1/|x_i − x_j| ≫ K²·log K/H, the one-dimensional Thomson (Coulomb energy) bound, while with all gaps ≍ H/K the consecutive-gap sum is only ≍ K²/H. So a cluster of about K nearly equally spaced prime factors of n gains a factor log K. Tao realised this with n = ∏(t − h_i) over a well-spaced prime tuple, which needs the prime k-tuples conjecture. Larsen made it unconditional by settling for a short cluster of K ≈ log t/(log* t·log log t) primes in [t, 2t], pairwise at least ε·log t apart inside a window of length O(K·log t), found by Brun's sieve and pigeonhole; this gives T ≫ 1/log* t and S = o(T) at one scale. Multiplying such clusters at tower-type scales t_i = exp(t_{i−1}) accumulates T ≈ Σ 1/(i + const) = ∞, while cross-scale contributions to S stay negligible.

**Main steps:**
- Energy bound: K points in an interval of length H have all-pairs sum ≫ K²·log K/H. If the gaps are at least N, the consecutive sum is at most K/N.
- One scale (Tao's proposition, in Larsen's variant): if the prime factors of n are t + h₁ < ⋯ < t + h_K with the h_i O(log t)-separated and (2(h_K − h₁))^K = o(t), then consecutive divisors that are not both primes contribute O(1/log log t) to S. Products of equally many primes that differ in a non-constant way are far apart, and near-collisions are counted directly.
- Existence without conjectures: Brun's upper-bound sieve limits the pairs of primes in [t, 2t] at distance less than ε·log t. Pigeonhole over windows of length H = 8K·log t then finds K primes pairwise at least ε·log t apart, so T(B) ≫ 1/log* t and S = o(T).
- Many scales: take prime sets B_i at t_i = exp(t_{i−1}) and n_i = ∏_{j≤i} ∏B_j. Divisors that mix scales have large gaps and contribute o(1), so S(div n_i) ≪ Σ_j S(div m_j) = o(Σ_j T(div m_j)), while T(div n_i) ≥ Σ_j T(div m_j) ≍ Σ_j 1/(j + log* t₀) → ∞.
- So the ratio T/(1 + S) is unbounded.

**A hit looks like:** the notes take n to be a product of primes clustered in a short window with roughly equal spacing, a perturbed arithmetic progression of prime factors, and use a Coulomb or Thomson-type lower bound for the all-pairs sum against the consecutive-gap sum. For the unconditional version, they get such clusters by a sieve or pigeonhole argument and stack them over many widely separated scales so the all-pairs sum diverges.

**A partial hit looks like:** Tao's conditional disproof via prime k-tuples alone, which has the right mechanism, although the formal statement needs an unconditional proof; numerical evidence from highly composite numbers or factorials; or the upper bound LHS ≪ (log τ(n))·RHS without a construction.

**Confidence:** high. I read both write-ups (Tao's note and Larsen's paper); Tao endorsed Larsen's proof on the forum, and it has since been formalised.
