# 259

Is Σ μ(n)²·n/2ⁿ, the sum of n/2ⁿ over squarefree n, irrational?

**Answer:** true

**Sources:** erdosproblems.com/259 (cached page); forum thread /forum/thread/259: Tao (2 Sep 2025) found Chen–Ruzsa through OEIS A371134, and ster (21 Apr 2026) formalised it with Aristotle; proof-claims page (empty); the Lean formalisation linked there (gist ster-oc/Erdos259.lean), whose lemmas are labelled as Chen–Ruzsa's Lemma 1, Lemma 3 and Theorem 4 and whose parameter choices I read; the site's bibliography entry [ChRu99], Chen–Ruzsa, "On the irrationality of certain series", Period. Math. Hungar. 38 (1999) 31–37. I did not read the 1999 paper itself.

**Key idea:** This is Chen–Ruzsa's criterion for series Σ a_n·l^{−a_n} over distinct positive integers a_n: cut the series at c = h·l^m. Then Σ_{a_n>c} c·l^{c−a_n} = h·Σ_{a_n>c} l^{m+c−a_n} is an integer (the terms with a_n ≤ c+m) plus h·Σ_{a_n>c+m} l^{−(a_n−c−m)}, so E = Σ_{a_n>c} (a_n−c)·l^{−(a_n−c)} + h·Σ_{a_n>c+m} l^{−(a_n−c−m)} satisfies u·E ∈ ℤ and E > 0 whenever the series equals v/u. E is tiny when no a_n lies in either of the two windows (c, c+k] and (c+m, c+m+k+h]. For squarefree a_n (more generally r-free or squarefull a_n), such windows exist for every k, because the Chinese remainder theorem can make every integer in them divisible by the square of some prime.

**Main steps:**
- Rewrite the series as Σ_j s_j/2^{s_j} over the squarefree numbers s_j, which are distinct and 2-free.
- Criterion (Chen–Ruzsa Lemma 1): if the series equals v/u, then for c = h·2^m the quantity E above is positive with u·E ∈ ℤ, so it suffices to make E → 0 along a sequence of choices of (h, m).
- If no squarefree number lies in (c, c+k], the first part of E is at most Σ_{j>k} j·2^{−j}. If none lies in (c+m, c+m+k+h], the second part is at most h·Σ_{j>k+h} 2^{−j}. Both tend to 0 as k → ∞, uniformly in h.
- Build the windows with CRT over distinct primes. First choose h (and a period M) so that h·2^m + j is divisible by a prime square for 1 ≤ j ≤ k whenever M | m, using that 2^m is periodic modulo prime squares. Then choose such an m so that h·2^m + m + j is also divisible by a prime square for 1 ≤ j ≤ k + h.
- The second window mixes exponential and linear terms in m. Chen–Ruzsa's Lemma 3 solves a·l^m + m + b ≡ 0 (mod q^r) with m ≡ 0 (mod q−1). A prime sequence with p_{i+1} ≡ −1 (mod p₀⋯p_i) and gcd(p_i − 1, p_j) = 1 keeps the congruences on m compatible. Then u·E is an integer strictly between 0 and 1, a contradiction.

**A hit looks like:** the notes contain an irrationality argument that multiplies by l^c with c of the special form h·l^m, or an equivalent device, to cancel the linear growth of the numerators n in n/2ⁿ. It is paired with a CRT construction of long runs of non-squarefree integers placed both just after c and just after c + m. Citing Chen–Ruzsa's theorem (Σ a_n·l^{−a_n} is irrational when the a_n are distinct and each is r-free or squarefull) and applying it also counts.

**A partial hit looks like:** the notes see that long runs of non-squarefree integers exist by CRT and try a tail or binary-expansion argument, but miss that after a plain cutoff c the tail is about c in size because of the numerators, so no small integer appears. Also partial: proving the easy variant Σ μ(n)²/2ⁿ, whose binary digits are not eventually periodic.
