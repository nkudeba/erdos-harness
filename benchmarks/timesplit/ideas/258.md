# 258

For every sequence of positive integers a_n → ∞, is the series Σ τ(n)/(a₁a₂⋯a_n) irrational, where τ(n) is the number of divisors of n?

**Answer:** true

**Sources:** erdosproblems.com/258 (cached page); forum thread /forum/thread/258: Chojecki's announcement of the GPT-5.4 Pro note (14 Apr 2026), Sothanaphan's and Tao's comments on the same day, and ster's Lean gist, which is conditional on Tao–Teräväinen; proof-claims page (empty); Tao–Teräväinen, arXiv:2512.01739 (abstract); pool.json.

**Key idea:** The input is Tao–Teräväinen's theorem that there are infinitely many N with Ω(N+k) ≪ k for every k ≥ 1, which answers an Erdős–Straus question; it gives τ(N+k) ≤ 2^{Ω(N+k)} ≤ 2^{Ck} with C absolute. For such N the tail of the series beyond the N-th term decays geometrically once the a_j are large. That feeds the standard irrationality argument: if the sum were p/q, then q·a₁⋯a_N times the tail would be a positive integer, and yet it would be less than 1.

**Main steps:**
- Suppose Σ τ(n)/(a₁⋯a_n) = p/q. Multiplying by q·a₁⋯a_N and removing the integer partial sum leaves T_N = q·Σ_{k≥1} τ(N+k)/(a_{N+1}⋯a_{N+k}), so T_N is a positive integer for every N.
- Tao–Teräväinen, using a Maynard-type sieve and the probabilistic method, give infinitely many N with Ω(N+k) ≤ Ck for all k ≥ 1, hence τ(N+k) ≤ 2^{Ck}.
- Because a_n → ∞, for large such N every a_{N+j} with j ≥ 1 is at least 2^{C+1}q.
- Then T_N ≤ q·Σ_k (2^C/(2^{C+1}q))^k < 1, a contradiction. No monotonicity of a_n is needed; the 1971 Erdős–Straus proof needed it.

**A hit looks like:** the notes reduce irrationality to a uniform bound τ(N+k) ≤ 2^{Ck}, or Ω(N+k) ≪ k, holding for all k simultaneously along infinitely many N, and they cite or prove it (Tao–Teräväinen). The tail argument alone is routine. The hit is seeing that this uniform bound over consecutive integers is exactly what the argument needs, and knowing where it comes from.

**A partial hit looks like:** the standard tail argument with the obstruction named: a large τ(N+1) can beat a slowly growing a_{N+1}. Also partial: the monotone case (Erdős–Straus 1971), the related constant-base case Σ τ(n)/tⁿ (Erdős 1948), or bounds on τ(N+k) only for small k or only on average.
