# 1148

Can every large integer n be written as x² + y² − z² with max(x², y², z²) ≤ n?

**Answer:** true

**Sources:** erdosproblems.com/1148 (cached page); forum thread /forum/thread/1148: Chojecki's announcement (16 Mar 2026), Tao's context on Linnik, Duke and ELMV (17 Mar), Chojecki's conditional Lean check, which takes Duke's theorem for granted (17 Mar), and later refinements to the constant 1/2 + ε (May 2026); proof-claims page (empty); Chojecki, "Bounded representations by x² + y² − z²" (ulam.ai/research/erdos1148-full.pdf, linked from the forum; full text read); Einsiedler–Lindenstrauss–Michel–Venkatesh, Enseign. Math. 58 (2012), as used in that note.

**Key idea:** Change variables to (a, b, c) = (x + z, 2y, z − x). Then x² + y² − z² = (b² − 4ac)/4, so representations of n correspond to binary quadratic forms [a, b, c] of discriminant 4n with a ≡ c (mod 2), and the size condition says (a, b, c)/(2√n) lies in a fixed bounded region K of the hyperboloid B² − 4AC = 1. Duke's theorem, in the equidistribution form given by ELMV for positive non-square discriminants, implies that every fixed nonempty, relatively compact open patch of that hyperboloid contains a scaled primitive form of discriminant d, for all large non-square d. Choosing a patch Ω ⊂ K that stays inside K under two SL₂(ℤ) moves fixes the parity of a and c, and square n = m² are trivial (m² + 0² − 0²).

**Main steps:**
- Dictionary: triples (a, b, c) with b² − 4ac = 4n, b even and a ≡ c (mod 2) correspond to (x, y, z) = ((a − c)/2, b/2, (a + c)/2) with x² + y² − z² = n.
- Region: K = {B² − 4AC = 1 : |A − C| < 1, |B| < 1, |A + C| < 1} is open and relatively compact, and its points give |x|, |y|, |z| < √n.
- Parity: discriminant 4n forces b even. If a ≢ c (mod 2), apply T (q(u, v) ↦ q(u + v, v)) or U = T∘S. Take a patch Ω around P₀ = (2/5, −2/5, −21/40) with Ω, T(Ω) and U(Ω) all inside K.
- Equidistribution: ELMV's theorem says the measures on closed geodesics of discriminant d converge weak-* to Haar measure on PGL₂(ℤ)\PGL₂(ℝ). Through a point-counting corollary, this gives a primitive form of discriminant 4n whose scaled point lies in Ω, for all large non-square n.
- Squares are handled directly, so every large n is representable. The argument is qualitative and gives no explicit threshold.
