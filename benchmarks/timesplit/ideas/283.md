# 283

For an integer-valued polynomial p with positive leading coefficient and no common divisor d ≥ 2 of all its values, is every large m equal to p(n₁) + ⋯ + p(n_k) for some distinct n_i with Σ 1/n_i = 1?

**Answer:** true

**Sources:** erdosproblems.com/283 (cached page); forum thread /forum/thread/283: Price's announcement of the GPT-5.5 Pro proof (3 May 2026), Sothanaphan's summary (3 May), Bloom's detailed summary (10 May), and paws's Lean formalisation (6 May), which uses Graham's 1964 theorem, first as an axiom and then formalised for the needed case; proof-claims page (empty); Graham, "Complete sequences of polynomial values", Duke Math. J. 31 (1964), as described on the forum; pool.json.

**Key idea:** The key is local switching. Replacing one denominator D by the three denominators 2D, 3D and 6D keeps Σ 1/n_i unchanged, since 1/D = 1/(2D) + 1/(3D) + 1/(6D), and changes the p-sum by the fixed polynomial amount q(D) = p(2D) + p(3D) + p(6D) − p(D). Apply this to a telescoping family D_j = u_j·u_{j+1} with u_j = 36j + 1, where Σ_{j<J} 1/D_j = (1 − 1/u_J)/36. The switch amounts Q(j) = q(D_j) then form a polynomial sequence of degree 2·deg p with positive leading coefficient. Graham's 1964 completeness theorem says every large multiple of some fixed g is a sum of distinct Q(j), so switching a suitable subset of the D_j adds any required amount to a base representation.

**Main steps:**
- Base representations: 1 = Σ_{j<J} 1/D_j + 1/(36·u_J) + Σ_{a∈A_k} 1/a. Here A_k is a fixed finite set with reciprocal sum 35/36 whose elements avoid the residues 0, 1, 2, 3, 6 mod 36, so no denominators collide after switching, and whose p-sum is ≡ k (mod g). Finding such an A_k for every residue k is a finite check that uses the no-common-divisor hypothesis.
- The base p-sums N_J^k grow like c·J^{2d}, with N_{J+1}^k − N_J^k = p(D_J) + O(J^d), so they are spaced polynomially and each residue class mod g is available.
- Graham 1964: there are g and X such that every multiple of g that is at least X is a sum of distinct values Q(j).
- For a target m ≡ k (mod g), pick J with m − N_J^k at least X and of order J^{2d}. Write m − N_J^k = Σ_{j∈S} Q(j) with S ⊂ {0, …, J−1}, and switch D_j → {2D_j, 3D_j, 6D_j} for each j ∈ S.
- The result has distinct denominators, reciprocal sum 1 and p-sum m. The same scheme proves the stronger version with 1 replaced by any positive rational α.

They combine it with a completeness theorem for sums of distinct polynomial values (Graham 1964, Roth–Szekeres, Cassels) applied to the switch polynomial rather than to p itself.
