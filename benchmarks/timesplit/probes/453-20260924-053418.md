## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this has been resolved, with the answer **no**.

I attribute the result to Carl Pomerance, in "The prime number graph" (Math. Comp., 1979). My recollection is that he proved there are infinitely many $n$ such that
$$p_n^2 > p_{n+i}\,p_{n-i} \quad \text{for all } 0 < i < n.$$
If that is right, the claim "for all sufficiently large $n$ some $i$ works" is false.

Key idea, as I recall it:
- Take logarithms and look at the points $(k, \log p_k)$.
- The condition $p_n^2 > p_{n+i}p_{n-i}$ for every $i$ says that $\log p_n$ lies strictly above every symmetric chord through $n$.
- Pomerance studied the convex hull of the "prime number graph."
- He showed that infinitely many of these points are vertices of the upper convex hull, i.e. extreme points. Such $n$ automatically satisfy the inequality for all $i$.
- The proof combines the fact that $\log p_k$ is roughly concave (from the prime number theorem) with a hull-vertex argument that controls local irregularities of the primes.

I am fairly confident in the attribution and the direction. I am less sure of the exact technical details, and of whether erdosproblems.com lists the problem with exactly this resolution.

**2. Guess.** Not needed, since I recall a resolution. If I were wrong about the recollection, "no" would still be my guess, on the convex-hull heuristic above.

**3. Source.** C. Pomerance, "The prime number graph," Math. Comp. 33 (1979). I do not recall a formal proof of it.
