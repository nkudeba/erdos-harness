## Answer

1. **Status: I believe it is resolved, in the affirmative (yes).** Here $G_k$ is the 1-subdivision of $K_k$: each edge of $K_k$ is replaced by a path of length 2.
   - Conlon and Lee proved that $\mathrm{ex}(n, K_k') = O(n^{3/2-\delta_k})$ for some $\delta_k>0$. The paper is "On the extremal number of subdivisions" (IMRN, around 2021; arXiv 2018/2019).
   - Their key idea is a dependent-random-choice style argument. It shows that a graph with about $n^{3/2-\delta}$ edges and no such subdivision would contain too many $C_4$'s, or too-regular common-neighbourhood structure, in a way that yields a contradiction.
   - Oliver Janzer later improved the exponent saving to $\delta_k = 1/(4k-6)$, in "Improved bounds for the extremal number of subdivisions." I think this bound is tight for $k=3$, since $G_3$ is $C_6$ and $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_6)=\Theta(n^{4/3})$.

2. This is not just a guess; I recall the resolution. I am not fully certain that erdosproblems.com lists #1021 as solved, though I believe it does.

3. The papers I recall are Conlon–Lee, "On the extremal number of subdivisions," and Janzer, "Improved bounds for the extremal number of subdivisions."
