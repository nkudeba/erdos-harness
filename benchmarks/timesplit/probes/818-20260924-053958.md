## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this has been resolved affirmatively, in a stronger form than asked. Mei-Chu Chang's Annals of Mathematics paper (2003), "The Erdős–Szemerédi problem on sum set and product set", proves (as I recall it) the following. If $A \subset \mathbb{Z}$ is finite and $|A+A| \le K|A|$, then $|AA| \ge c(K)|A|^2$. I remember $c(K)$ as being of the form $36^{-K}$, though I am unsure of that exact constant. Either way, no power of $\log|A|$ is lost.

   The key idea, as I recall it:
   - Small doubling plus Freiman's theorem puts $A$ inside a generalized arithmetic progression of bounded dimension.
   - One then looks at the multiplicative structure through prime factorizations.
   - A combinatorial argument, roughly a multiplicative energy or product-counting bound, shows that sets in such progressions have almost no multiplicative coincidences. So $AA$ is essentially as large as $|A|^2$.

2. **Expected answer.** This is not purely a guess, since I recall the resolution: the answer is **yes**.

3. **Source.** M.-C. Chang, "The Erdős–Szemerédi problem on sum set and product set", Ann. of Math. 157 (2003), 939–957. I believe erdosproblems.com lists #818 as solved by this paper. I do not recall a formal (Lean) proof.
