## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe it has been resolved, in the affirmative (yes). My recollection is that R. Tijdeman proved it in "On a conjecture of Turán and Erdős" (Indagationes Mathematicae, around 1966). The question comes out of Turán's work on power sums.

   I don't reliably remember the key idea. The natural approach would go like this:
   - Each block of n−1 consecutive vanishing power sums gives a linear system in the z_i, like a Vandermonde system.
   - Having infinitely many such blocks forces strong algebraic constraints, such as all |z_i| = 1 and the z_i being roots of unity.
   - This leads to the conclusion that the z_i are the n-th roots of unity.

   That outline is a plausible reconstruction, not something I recall from the paper.

2. **Expected answer.** Not applicable as a pure guess, since I think I remember the result. If my memory is wrong, I would still guess "yes", because the statement is the natural rigidity statement for Turán-type power sums.
