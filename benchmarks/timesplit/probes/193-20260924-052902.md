## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I know, this problem is still **open**. I do not know of any resolution.

   What I recall as background, with some uncertainty about the details:
   - Gerver and Ramsey (1979) showed that in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ every such walk contains infinitely many collinear points.
   - In $\mathbb{Z}^3$ they showed that some walk avoids a larger number of collinear points. I believe that number is five, but I am not sure. Either way, the question for three points was left open.

2. **Expected answer (a guess).** I would lean towards **yes**: three collinear points must occur. My reasons are weak:
   - A bounded-step walk revisits a small set of step directions very densely.
   - Avoiding even three collinear points among infinitely many lattice points seems much harder than avoiding four or five.

   The Gerver–Ramsey type constructions show that $\mathbb{Z}^3$ has real room for collinearity avoidance, though, so "no" is quite plausible. This is a low-confidence guess.
