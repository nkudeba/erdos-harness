## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this is resolved, with the answer **yes**.

   My recollection is that József Beck settled it around 1981. The source I have in mind is "Balancing families of integer sequences" in *Combinatorica*. As I remember it, he built a colouring f:ℕ→{−1,1} for which the partial sums along the first d sequences stay bounded by a function of d alone. I think the bound was polynomial in d, possibly around d^{4+ε}.

   The key idea, as far as I recall, is an iterative or partial-colouring construction. The integers are processed in blocks, and the colouring is chosen so that discrepancy along each A_i is kept under control. The allowed error grows with the index i, so infinitely many sequences can be handled at once while each fixed d gets a uniform bound. This is in the spirit of Beck–Fiala-type discrepancy arguments.

   I may be misremembering later improvements to the dependence on d, and I am not certain of the exact bound.
