## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this is resolved, with answer **yes**. As I recall, the erdosproblems.com page marks it as proved and attributes it to D. K. L. Shiu's theorem on strings of congruent primes (J. London Math. Soc., 2000). That theorem says that for any coprime $a, d$ there are arbitrarily long runs of consecutive primes all congruent to $a \pmod d$.

   The argument I can reconstruct is:
   - Fix $n$ and $d$, and write $S_j = p_{n+1} + \cdots + p_{n+j}$.
   - By Shiu's theorem, some later stretch of $d$ consecutive primes $p_{n+j+1}, \dots, p_{n+j+d}$ are all $\equiv 1 \pmod d$.
   - Then $S_j, S_{j+1}, \dots, S_{j+d}$ run through consecutive residues mod $d$.
   - So one of them is $\equiv 0 \pmod d$, which gives the required $k$.

   The derivation is solid. What I am less sure of is exactly how the site records the attribution and status.
