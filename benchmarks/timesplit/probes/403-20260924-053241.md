## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this has been resolved, in the affirmative: there are only finitely many solutions. My recollection is that Frankl and Lin proved this around 1976. They showed that the largest solution is 2^7 = 128 = 2! + 3! + 5!. The small solutions are 2^0 = 0!, 2^1 = 0! + 1!, 2^2 = 0! + 1! + 2!, 2^3 = 2! + 3! and 2^7 = 2! + 3! + 5!, with ambiguity depending on whether 0! and 1! both count.

   I recall the key idea only vaguely. It is a 2-adic and congruence argument. The smallest factorials in the sum fix its low-order 2-adic behaviour. Since n! is divisible by a large power of 2 once n is large, the sum must match 2^m modulo high powers of 2 (and possibly other moduli). That forces a contradiction beyond small m.

2. **Expected answer.** Not needed as a guess, since I believe it is resolved. If I am wrong about the resolution, I would still expect "yes, finitely many" on heuristic and congruence grounds.
