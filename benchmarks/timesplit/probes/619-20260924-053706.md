## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I know, this problem is still open. I do not recall a resolution.

   The problem comes from Erdős, Gyárfás and Ruszinkó, "How to decrease the diameter of triangle-free graphs" (Combinatorica, 1998). From memory, which I have not checked:
   - They proved general bounds for h₃ and h₄.
   - They showed that h₅(G) < (1−c)n for some constant c > 0.
   - They left open whether the same holds for diameter 4.

   I do not remember any later paper, preprint or formal proof that settles the diameter-4 case.

2. **Guess (not a remembered result): yes.** A linear saving holds for diameter 5, so it seems plausible it also holds for diameter 4. The natural approach is to take a spanning tree and add edges to a cleverly chosen set of vertices, saving a constant fraction of the n edges. I hold this weakly.

3. **Source that settles it:** none that I recall. The only source I can name is the 1998 Erdős–Gyárfás–Ruszinkó paper, which poses the question rather than answering it.
