## Answer

1. **Status: I believe it is resolved, in the affirmative (yes), at least for all sufficiently large n.**
   - I recall that Keevash and Sudakov proved the bound: in any 2-colouring of the edges of $K_n$, at most $n^2/4$ edges lie in no monochromatic triangle.
   - I believe this is in their paper on edges not covered by monochromatic copies of a fixed graph, around 2004 in J. Combin. Theory Ser. B.
   - The bound is sharp. Colour a complete bipartite graph $K_{n/2,n/2}$ with one colour and both halves with the other. Then no cross edge is in a monochromatic triangle.
   - I think their result covers the more general question for $K_k$, with Turán-type bounds.
   - Later work, for example by Jie Ma, treated related questions for other fixed graphs.
   - I am not certain whether their proof covers every $n$ or only $n$ large enough.
   - I only vaguely recall the key idea: an extremal or stability-type argument about the structure of the graph of uncovered edges, compared against the Turán bound. I cannot reconstruct the proof reliably.
