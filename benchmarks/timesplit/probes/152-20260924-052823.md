**1. Status.** I don't reliably know. My best recollection is that erdosproblems.com lists #152 as open. I also have a vague sense that there has been recent activity on the isolated-sums question for Sidon sets, possibly in 2025 during the wave of work on Erdős problems. I cannot name a specific result, author or date, so I will not claim a resolution.

**2. Expected answer (guess): yes.**
- If $A$ is Sidon with $|A| = n$, then $A+A$ has about $n^2/2$ elements. These lie in an interval of length about $2\max A$.
- For a dense Sidon set, $\max A \approx n^2$. The sumset then occupies roughly a quarter of its interval.
- Heuristically, a positive proportion of those sums should be isolated, meaning neither neighbour is in $A+A$.
- To avoid isolated sums, the sums would have to cluster into runs. That seems to need additive structure that the Sidon condition forbids.
- So I expect the number of isolated elements to tend to infinity, and likely to grow like a power of $|A|$.
- The hard part of a proof is probably the case where $A+A$ is very dense in its interval, since that is where isolation is hardest to force.
