## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this has been resolved, with a **yes** answer. My recollection is a 2025 arXiv paper by **Wouter van Doorn and Vjekoslav Kovač**. As I recall, they construct a lacunary sequence $A$ such that every rational in some open interval is a finite sum of distinct reciprocals from $A$.

   I only remember the key idea in outline, and I may be misremembering details. The construction works in blocks. The early elements supply enough subset sums to approximate any target rational in the interval. Later elements are chosen with special divisibility structure, built from Egyptian-fraction style identities, so that the remaining error can always be cancelled exactly. The whole sequence keeps a fixed growth ratio $\lambda > 1$. This matters because rationals are countable, so measure-type obstructions to lacunary subset sums do not rule it out. I don't reliably recall how large $\lambda$ can be taken.

2. **If I am wrong about the resolution.** I would still guess **yes**, for the same countability reason. This part is a guess.

3. **Source.** I recall the paper as roughly "Lacunary sequences whose reciprocal sums represent all rationals in an interval" by van Doorn and Kovač (arXiv, 2025). I'm fairly but not fully sure of the title and the attribution. I don't recall a formal Lean proof.
