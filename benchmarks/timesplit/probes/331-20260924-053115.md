## Answer

## 1. Status

To my knowledge the problem is **resolved, with answer no**. I believe this is the entry erdosproblems.com lists as disproved. I attribute the construction to Ruzsa, but I'm less sure of that attribution and I don't remember a date.

The construction I recall:

- Let **A** be the numbers of the form $\sum \varepsilon_i 4^i$ with each $\varepsilon_i \in \{0,1\}$. These are the numbers whose binary digits are nonzero only in even positions.
- Let **B = 2A**. These are the numbers whose binary digits are nonzero only in odd positions.

**Density.** Both sets satisfy $|A \cap [1,N]| \asymp N^{1/2}$ and $|B \cap [1,N]| \asymp N^{1/2}$. A counting set bounded by some $N^{1/2}$-type quantity oscillates by a constant factor, so this should still meet the $\gg N^{1/2}$ condition for all large $N$. I haven't checked the constants.

**No nontrivial solutions.** Suppose $a_1 - a_2 = b_1 - b_2$. Rearranging gives $\sum_j c_j 2^j = 0$, where:

- the even positions $j$ carry digits $\delta_i \in \{-1,0,1\}$ from $A - A$;
- the odd positions carry digits $-\eta_i \in \{-1,0,1\}$ from $B - B$.

If some $c_j$ is nonzero, take the lowest such $j$. Then the sum is congruent to $2^j \pmod{2^{j+1}}$, so it is not 0, a contradiction. Hence every $c_j = 0$, which forces $a_1 - a_2 = 0$.

So the only common difference of $A - A$ and $B - B$ is 0, and there are no solutions with $a_1 - a_2 = b_1 - b_2 \neq 0$.
