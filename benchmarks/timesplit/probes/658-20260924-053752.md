## Answer

1. **Status: I believe this is resolved, and the answer is yes.** I'm recalling this rather than proving it here.

   - Even if "square" means only axis-parallel squares, the vertices form the pattern {(x,y), (x+d,y), (x,y+d), (x+d,y+d)} with d ≥ 1.
   - That is a fixed finite configuration in ℤ², so it falls under the multidimensional Szemerédi theorem of Furstenberg and Katznelson (1978).
   - That theorem says every positive-density subset of {1,…,N}² contains a homothetic copy (with positive scaling factor d) of any fixed finite pattern, once N is large enough depending on δ.
   - The proof goes through the Furstenberg correspondence principle and ergodic theory: multiple recurrence for commuting measure-preserving transformations.
   - Tilted squares only make the conclusion easier to reach, since axis-parallel squares already suffice.
   - Later work gave combinatorial proofs, via hypergraph regularity (Gowers; Rödl–Schacht–Nagle–Skokan) and density Hales–Jewett. These give quantitative, but very weak, bounds.
   - I don't reliably remember what erdosproblems.com lists as the resolving reference. I also don't know whether there has been any recent improvement to the quantitative bounds for squares.

2. This is not just a guess, although I'm unsure how the website phrases its entry. If the problem is indeed the one stated, I'm fairly confident the answer is yes.

3. **Source:** Furstenberg–Katznelson, "An ergodic Szemerédi theorem for commuting transformations" (J. Analyse Math., 1978). I don't remember a specific formal proof.
