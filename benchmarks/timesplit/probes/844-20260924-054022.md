## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this has been resolved in the affirmative: the extremal set is the even numbers together with the odd non-squarefree numbers. My memory is that it was settled fairly recently, around 2025, possibly with AI or formal-proof assistance, and recorded on erdosproblems.com. I cannot reliably name the authors or give the exact date.

On the method, the only piece I am sure of is the setup, which is elementary:

- If $a$ is not squarefree, then $ab$ is never squarefree, so all non-squarefree numbers can always be included.
- The squarefree elements of $A$ must then pairwise share a prime factor, and none can be $1$. So they form an intersecting family of sets of prime divisors.
- The problem becomes showing that no intersecting family of squarefree numbers $\le N$ beats "all even squarefree numbers".

I believe the published argument proves that last step by some shifting or compression comparison against the prime $2$, but I don't actually remember it.

**2. Expected answer.** Setting aside what I think I remember, I would still guess "yes". Among "star" families (all squarefree multiples of one fixed prime), the multiples of $2$ are the most numerous. Non-star intersecting families should lose density.

**3. Specific source.** I have no citation I trust. I have only a vague recollection that the erdosproblems.com page lists it as solved.
