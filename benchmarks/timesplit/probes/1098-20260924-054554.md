## Answer

**1. Status: resolved, answer yes.** As far as I recall, B. H. Neumann settled this in 1976, in "A problem of Paul Erdős on groups" (J. Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A 21 (1976)). He showed that if a group has no infinite set of pairwise non-commuting elements, then the centre $Z(G)$ has finite index in $G$.

The bound then follows directly. Two elements in the same coset of $Z(G)$ commute, so a clique in $\Gamma$ contains at most one element from each coset. Every clique therefore has size at most $[G:Z(G)]$.

Key idea, as I remember it: an infinite Ramsey-type argument. Neumann shows that if the centre had infinite index, one could build an infinite family of pairwise non-commuting elements. The underlying lemma is his result that a group covered by finitely many cosets of subgroups is covered by those cosets whose subgroups have finite index. I am confident about the result and the author, but less certain about the exact details of the proof.

**2.** Not a guess; see point 1.

**3.** Yes: B. H. Neumann, "A problem of Paul Erdős on groups," J. Austral. Math. Soc. 21 (1976), 467–472. I am fairly, but not completely, sure of the page numbers. I do not recall a formal (Lean) proof of it.
