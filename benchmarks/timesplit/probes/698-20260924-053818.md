## Answer

1. **Status: I believe it is resolved, with answer yes.** My recollection is that George Bergman settled it in "On common divisors of multinomial coefficients" (Bull. Austral. Math. Soc., around 2011). I think he proved that for 2 ≤ i < j ≤ n/2 there is an absolute constant c > 0 with

   gcd(C(n,i), C(n,j)) ≥ c · n^{1/2} · 2^i / i^{3/2}.

   Since the right-hand side tends to infinity with n, this gives h(n) → ∞. I am fairly but not fully sure of the exact form of the bound.

   The key idea I recall is a standard identity and then a finer argument:
   - The identity C(n,j)·C(j,i) = C(n,i)·C(n−i, j−i) means C(n,i) divides C(n,j)·C(j,i).
   - So gcd(C(n,i), C(n,j)) ≥ C(n,i)/C(j,i).
   - This crude bound can be small when j is large relative to i. Bergman's work, which I believe uses gcd structure of multinomial coefficients and a p-adic (Kummer-type) analysis, handles those cases.
   - Erdős and Szekeres had earlier shown only that the gcd is greater than 1.

2. Not a guess. If I am wrong about the resolution, I would still expect "yes", because the identity above already gives large lower bounds in most ranges of i and j.

3. The source I recall is G. M. Bergman, "On common divisors of multinomial coefficients", Bull. Aust. Math. Soc. (c. 2011), cited on the erdosproblems page.
