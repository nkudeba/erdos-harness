## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this is resolved, and the answer is **yes**. My memory of the source is moderately confident, not certain.

- **Who and when:** S. Akiyama and V. Komornik, *"Discrete spectra and Pisot numbers"*, J. Number Theory, around 2013.
- **Result:** as I recall it, they proved that $x_{k+1}-x_k\to 0$ for all $1<q\le 2^{1/4}\approx 1.189$. This answers the Erdős–Joó–Komornik question positively, with $\epsilon = 2^{1/4}-1$ sufficing.
- **Key idea (as I recall it):** analyse the set of digit patterns realisable by the sums. For $q$ that small, the lattice-like structure of the sums lets one exhibit arbitrarily small positive differences uniformly along the whole sequence, not just somewhere. I remember the statement better than the details of the argument.
- **Why smallness of $\epsilon$ is needed:** if $q$ is a Pisot number, the difference of two sums is a $\{-1,0,1\}$-combination of powers of $q$. That set is uniformly discrete, so the gaps stay bounded below. The smallest Pisot number is about $1.3247$, so $\epsilon$ must be below about $0.3247$.
- **Later work:** I recall D.-J. Feng extending this kind of result toward $q<\sqrt2$. I am less sure of the exact statement.

**2. Guess.** Not applicable, since I recall a resolution. If I am misremembering, I would still guess "yes", for the Pisot obstruction reason above.

**3. Source.** Akiyama–Komornik, "Discrete spectra and Pisot numbers". I do not know of a formal proof.
