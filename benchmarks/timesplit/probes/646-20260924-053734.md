## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this has been resolved, and the answer is **yes**. I recall it being settled by **Daniel Berend** in the late 1990s, in a paper in the *Journal of Number Theory*, roughly titled "On the parity of exponents in the factorization of n!" (about 1997).
   - He showed that for any finite set of primes there are infinitely many n with every v_{p_i}(n!) even.
   - I believe he also showed that such n occur with bounded gaps.
   - The starting point is Legendre's formula, v_p(n!) = (n − s_p(n))/(p−1), where s_p(n) is the base-p digit sum of n. This turns the condition into parity constraints on the base-p digits of n, for all the primes at once.
   - I recall the proof handling these constraints jointly with a combinatorial or dynamical recurrence argument. The idea is roughly that digit blocks in the different bases can be adjusted largely independently. I am not certain of the proof's details.
   - I also vaguely recall later refinements, such as density or counting results by Y.-G. Chen and coauthors, but I am unsure of the specifics.

2. **Expected answer.** Not needed as a guess, since I claim to remember a resolution. If my memory were wrong, I would still expect "yes" from heuristic independence of the parities across bases.

3. **Source.** D. Berend, *J. Number Theory* (circa 1997), on the parity of exponents in the prime factorization of n!. I am fairly but not fully sure of the exact title and year.
