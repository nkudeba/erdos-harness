## Answer

1. **Status: I believe it is resolved, and the answer is yes, t(n) = o(n).**
   - I recall that Noga Alon proved t(n) = O(n^{2/3} log n) in "Economical coverings of sets of lattice points" (Geom. Funct. Anal., 1991).
   - I also recall a lower bound of order n^{2/3}, attributed to Erdős. If both are right, t(n) is n^{2/3} up to a logarithmic factor, which is far below n.
   - I only remember the method loosely: I think the upper bound comes from a probabilistic, number-theoretic choice of points, arranged so that the lines through pairs of them hit every lattice point.
   - The lower bound is a counting argument over the ~t²/2 lines and the number of grid points each can cover. I do not remember the exact step that gets n^{2/3}.
