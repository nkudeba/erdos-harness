## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I recall, this problem has been **resolved, with answer yes**. Lorentz's general theorem on additive complements gives a set $A$ with $|A\cap[1,N]| \ll N\log\log N/\log N$ such that $A + \{2^k\}$ covers all large integers. Erdős asked whether the $\log\log N$ factor could be removed. I believe **Ruzsa** did remove it, constructing a set of density $\ll N/\log N$, in the early 1970s.

   On the method, I only have a vague memory. I think it is an explicit construction based on the binary digit structure of integers, choosing $a$ so that $n - a$ is a power of 2. I cannot reconstruct the details reliably.

2. **Expected answer.** Not applicable as a guess, since I believe it is resolved: yes. If my memory is wrong, "yes" is still the natural expectation. The bound $N/\log N$ is optimal up to constants, because there are only about $\log_2 N$ powers of 2 below $N$, so $A$ must have at least about $N/\log_2 N$ elements up to $N$.

3. **Source.** I think the reference is I. Z. Ruzsa, "On a problem of P. Erdős", Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 15 (1972). I am not fully certain of the title or year. I do not recall a formal proof.
