## Answer

1. **Status: I believe it is resolved, up to constant factors.**

   - **Upper bound.** Erdős (around 1963) showed H(n) ≪ n^{3/2} by taking f to be a random ±1 function. With positive probability, every subset X then has an edge-sum of size O(n^{3/2}), by a union bound over the 2^n subsets.
   - **Lower bound.** Erdős and Spencer ("Imbalances in k-colorations", Networks, 1971/72) proved the matching bound H(n) ≫ n^{3/2]. So H(n) ≍ n^{3/2}.
   - **Idea of the lower bound (as I recall it).** Take a random or well-chosen partition of the vertices into two sets A and B. Look at the signed sums of edges running between A and B. An averaging or second-moment argument, similar in spirit to a Cauchy–Schwarz or eigenvalue bound, shows that some X of the form A′ ∪ B′ has a sum of order n^{3/2}.
   - **What remains unknown.** The exact asymptotic constant, i.e. whether H(n)/n^{3/2} tends to a limit, is not determined as far as I know.
   - **Yes/no.** The question asks for an estimate, not a yes/no answer, so I don't give one.

2. This is not a guess about a hidden answer: I recall the order n^{3/2} as the established result. My uncertainty is about the exact attributions and dates, and about whether the problem page lists some refinement as open.

3. **Source:** P. Erdős and J. Spencer, "Imbalances in k-colorations", Networks 1 (1971/72). The upper bound is in Erdős's 1963 work on random constructions for tournaments and graphs. I don't know of a formal proof of either bound.
