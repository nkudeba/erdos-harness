## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this has been resolved in the affirmative ("yes"). My recollection is that erdosproblems.com attributes the solution to Balog and Wooley, in a paper from around 1998.

   As I remember it, their result says that for every ε > 0 and k ≥ 2, for all sufficiently large n, there are k consecutive integers up to n that are all n^ε-smooth.

   I only vaguely recall the key idea, and this part is uncertain. I think it is an explicit algebraic construction. You choose the integers as values of polynomials in a parameter, arranged so that each of the k consecutive values factors into many smaller polynomial values. A simple example of such a factorisation is x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1). Iterating or nesting this makes each factor small compared with n, so every integer in the string is n^ε-smooth. Choosing the parameter suitably then gives such strings at every sufficiently large scale n, not just along a sparse sequence.

   I am fairly confident that it is solved and that the answer is yes. I am less sure of the exact attribution, the year, and whether the published result covers "all sufficiently large n" or only infinitely many n.

2. **Expected answer.** Not needed as a pure guess, since I think I remember the result. If I am misremembering, I would still expect "yes": heuristically, smooth numbers with a fixed ε have positive density, so strings of k consecutive smooth numbers should exist.

3. **Source.** A. Balog and T. D. Wooley, "On strings of consecutive integers with no large prime factors," J. Austral. Math. Soc. (Series A), around 1998. I remember the paper's existence and approximate title, but not its details.
