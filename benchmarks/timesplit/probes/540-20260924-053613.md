## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this problem is resolved, and the answer is **yes**.

- It is the Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture on zero-sum subsets in $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$.
- For prime $N = p$, Olson (1968) proved it. He showed that any $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ with $|A| > 2\sqrt{p}$, roughly, has a non-empty zero-sum subset.
- For general $N$, Szemerédi (1970, "On a conjecture of Erdős and Heilbronn," Acta Arithmetica) proved that $|A| \ge c\sqrt{N}$ suffices, for some absolute constant $c$.
- I recall the key idea only vaguely, so treat this as approximate. The proofs grow the set of subset sums $\Sigma(A)$ iteratively. They use additive-combinatorial growth estimates of Cauchy–Davenport type. Szemerédi used a density-increment style argument to handle non-prime moduli.
- Later work refined the constant. Balandraud obtained essentially the sharp constant, about $\sqrt{2p}$, in the prime case. Other results cover general finite abelian groups.
- I believe erdosproblems.com lists #540 as solved, citing Szemerédi. I am not certain of the site's current wording.

**2.** This is not a guess; I am recalling the result above.

**3.** Sources I recall:
- Szemerédi, "On a conjecture of Erdős and Heilbronn," Acta Arith. 17 (1970).
- Olson, J. Number Theory (1968), for the prime case.
- Balandraud, for the sharp constant.
