## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I know, this has been **resolved in the affirmative (yes)**. I believe Saharon Shelah proved it in "Erdős and Rényi conjecture" (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 82 (1998), 179–185). The question goes back to Erdős and Rényi. Earlier partial results were due to Alon–Bollobás and to Erdős–Hajnal, and possibly Alon–Hajnal. Those earlier results gave bounds like $2^{n^{1/2}}$ or $2^{n/\mathrm{polylog}}$.

   I only vaguely remember the key idea. I think it goes roughly as follows:
   - Ramsey-type graphs (no homogeneous set of size $c\log n$) contain large structured pieces.
   - In these pieces, vertices are distinguishable by their neighbourhood patterns.
   - One then builds exponentially many induced subgraphs that cannot be isomorphic.

   I would not trust my recollection of the technical details.

2. **If it were not resolved.** My guess would still be "yes". Random-like graphs have very few automorphisms among their subgraphs, which suggests exponentially many non-isomorphic induced subgraphs.

3. **Source.** I recall S. Shelah, "Erdős and Rényi conjecture," JCTA 1998. I am fairly but not fully confident that this paper settles exactly this statement.
