## Answer

1. **Status:** I believe the problem is resolved, and the answer is **no**.
   - **Freud (around 1993)** constructed sets with no such consecutive sums of size about (19/36)N. Since 19/36 > 1/2, the bound N/2 + O(1) fails.
   - **Coppersmith and Phillips (around 1996)** improved the construction to about (13/24)N − O(1).
   - The same Coppersmith–Phillips work gives an upper bound of the form |A| ≤ (2/3 − ε)N. So the right constant, somewhere between 13/24 and 2/3, is still open, but the N/2 + O(1) question itself is settled negatively.
   - **Key idea:** explicit constructions that combine blocks of numbers in residue classes and intervals so that consecutive block sums avoid A while more than half the integers are kept. I don't remember the construction details.

2. This is not a guess. It is a recollection, though I'm unsure of the exact constants and years.

3. **Sources:** G. Freud, on sums of consecutive elements (early 1990s), and D. Coppersmith and S. Phillips, "On a question of Erdős on subsequence sums" (SIAM J. Discrete Math., around 1996). I believe the erdosproblems.com page lists #867 as disproved on this basis.
