## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I know, this is resolved, and the answer is **yes**. It was proved independently by Pollington (1979) and de Mathan (1980). For any lacunary sequence, the set of θ for which {‖θn_k‖} is not dense has Hausdorff dimension 1. In particular, such θ include irrational ones.

   - **Key idea:** a nested-intervals (Cantor-type) construction. Lacunarity means each new constraint ‖θn_k‖ ≥ δ removes only a small, controlled fraction of each interval surviving the earlier steps. So a Cantor set of θ remains that avoids a fixed neighbourhood of 0 for every k.
   - **Later refinements:**
     - Katznelson (2001) gave another proof.
     - Peres and Schlag, using the Lovász local lemma, gave a quantitative version: some θ has ‖θn_k‖ ≳ ε/log(1/ε) for all k.
     - Earlier partial results for the case of large lacunarity constant are due to Khintchine and Erdős.

2. This is not a guess. I recall the answer as yes.

3. Sources I recall:
   - A. D. Pollington, "On the density of sequence {n_kξ}", Illinois J. Math. 23 (1979).
   - B. de Mathan, "Numbers contravening a condition in density modulo 1", Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 36 (1980).
   - Y. Peres and W. Schlag, "Two Erdős problems on lacunary sequences: chromatic number and Diophantine approximation", Bull. LMS (2010).

   I am not aware of a formal proof.
