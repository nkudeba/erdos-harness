## Answer

1. **Status:** I believe this has been resolved, in the affirmative (**yes**). My recollection is that Yuhi Kamio settled it in a 2025 arXiv preprint on the asymptotics of infinite unit-fraction decompositions of 1. Stated precisely, the result is: if $a_1 < a_2 < \cdots$ has $\sum 1/a_k = 1$ and is not the Sylvester-type sequence $u_n + 1$, then $\liminf a_n^{1/2^n} < c_0$.

   The key idea, as I remember it, is a greedy-comparison argument. Define the partial remainders $1 - \sum_{k\le n} 1/a_k$. Once the sequence departs from the greedy (Sylvester) choice, these remainders carry a fixed multiplicative "deficit." That deficit forces infinitely many terms to grow noticeably more slowly in the doubly-exponential scale, which pushes the $\liminf$ strictly below $c_0$. I am not confident about the finer details of the proof.

2. **Expected answer:** Beyond my recollection, I would expect "yes" anyway. The greedy/Sylvester sequence is extremal for fast growth, and this is the standard heuristic (Erdős–Graham style).

3. **Source:** I have a moderately confident memory of Y. Kamio, "Asymptotic analysis of infinite decompositions of a unit fraction into unit fractions" (arXiv, 2025). I believe erdosproblems.com marks #315 as solved on this basis. I do not recall a formal proof in Lean.
