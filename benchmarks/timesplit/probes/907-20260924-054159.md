## Answer

1. **Resolved, answer yes.** To my knowledge this was settled by N. G. de Bruijn in 1951. He showed that the class of continuous functions has what is now called the *difference property*. If every difference $\Delta_h f(x) = f(x+h)-f(x)$ is continuous, then $f = g + h$ with $g$ continuous and $h$ additive.

   Restricting to $h>0$ loses nothing. For $h>0$, the function $f(x-h)-f(x) = -\big(f(y+h)-f(y)\big)$ with $y = x-h$ is also continuous. So every difference, for any real $h$, is continuous.

   I only roughly recall how the proof goes. The map $h \mapsto \Delta_h f$ satisfies the cocycle identity $\Delta_{h+k}f(x) = \Delta_h f(x+k) + \Delta_k f(x)$. One then uses a Hamel-basis / transfinite construction to build an additive $h$ so that $f - h$ is continuous. I cannot reconstruct the details with confidence.

   Kemperman and later Laczkovich extended the difference-property theory to other function classes. I believe erdosproblems.com lists #907 as solved by de Bruijn.

2. Not a guess: I recall the answer, though I am not certain every detail matches the website's statement exactly.

3. The paper is N. G. de Bruijn, "Functions whose differences belong to a given class", *Nieuw Archief voor Wiskunde* (2) 23 (1951), 194–218. I do not know of a formal proof.
