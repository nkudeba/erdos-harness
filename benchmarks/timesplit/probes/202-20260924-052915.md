## Answer

**1. Status.** As far as I know, this is still open in the sense that the exact order of growth is not settled. It is not a yes/no question, so there is no "yes" or "no" answer to report.

What I recall, with moderate confidence:
- **Erdős and Szemerédi** showed that the maximum $r$ is $o(N)$. More precisely, they gave bounds roughly of the form
  $$\frac{N}{\exp\big((\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\big)} \ll \max r \ll \frac{N}{(\log N)^{c}}.$$
- **de la Bretèche, Ford and Vandehey (≈2013)**, "On non-intersecting arithmetic progressions," improved this substantially. They showed that the maximum is
  $$\frac{N}{L(N)^{c+o(1)}}, \qquad L(N) = \exp\big(\sqrt{\log N \,\log\log N}\big),$$
  and I believe they pinned the constant $c$ down to a range, something like between $1/2$ and $\sqrt{2}$ (or similar).
- The key ideas are sieve and structure arguments on the moduli. Disjointness of the classes $a_i \pmod{n_i}$ and $a_j \pmod{n_j}$ forces $\gcd(n_i, n_j) \nmid a_i - a_j$. Constructions use moduli with suitably many small prime factors.

I am not certain of the exact constants in the exponent.

**2. Expectation (guess).** I expect the truth to be $N / L(N)^{c+o(1)}$ for some specific constant $c$, and that pinning down $c$ is what remains open.

**3. Source.** I recall the de la Bretèche–Ford–Vandehey paper on non-intersecting arithmetic progressions (Acta Arithmetica, around 2013), though not every detail. I recall no formal proof.
