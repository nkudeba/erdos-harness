## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this is resolved, and the answer is **no**. I associate the resolution with Alon's work on choice numbers from the early 1990s. The mechanism below I can check directly, so I am fairly confident in it. The exact attribution and wording are less certain.

The key idea is a simple example. Let G be the disjoint union of √n cliques, each of size √n.

- **G itself.** Every component is a clique on √n vertices, and cliques are chromatic-choosable. So χ_L(G) = √n.
- **The complement.** G^c is the complete multipartite graph with √n parts, each of size √n. The choice number of K_{m×k} (k parts of size m) is known to be Θ(k log m). Here that gives χ_L(G^c) = Θ(√n · log n).
- **The sum.** χ_L(G) + χ_L(G^c) = O(√n · log n). This is n^{1/2+o(1)}, so it is not larger than n^{1/2+c} for any fixed c > 0.

Alon also showed a lower bound of roughly the same order, about √n times a logarithmic factor. So the truth is n^{1/2+o(1)}, not n^{1/2+c}.

**2. Guess.** Not needed. I claim the answer "no" on the strength of the argument above, not as a guess.

**3. Source.** I recall N. Alon, "Choice numbers of graphs: a probabilistic approach" (Combinatorics, Probability and Computing, 1992), as the relevant paper. I also believe the erdosproblems.com page records the problem as disproved by Alon. I am not fully certain of either detail.
