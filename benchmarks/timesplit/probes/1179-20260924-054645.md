## Answer

**1. Status.** I don't know whether it has been resolved. I don't reliably remember any resolution.

- The problem traces back to Erdős–Rényi, "Probabilistic methods in group theory" (1965).
- I believe that line of work gives an upper bound of roughly $\log_2 N + O(\log\log N)$ in some form. I can't vouch for the exact form or for which groups it covers.
- The trivial lower bound is $g_\epsilon(N) \ge \log_2 N$, since you need $2^k \gtrsim N$ subsets to cover all $N$ targets.

I'm not confident whether the matching asymptotic $(1+o(1))\log_2 N$ has been established uniformly over all abelian groups.

**2. Guess: yes.**

- **Elementary abelian 2-groups.** Take $G=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^n$. If $A$ spans $G$, then every $g$ is hit exactly $2^{k-n}$ times. A random $A$ spans once $k = n + \omega(1)$, so there $g_\epsilon(N) = (1+o(1))\log_2 N$.
- **Other groups.** Heuristically, Fourier and second-moment arguments should give equidistribution once $k = \log_2 N + C_\epsilon \log\log N$ or similar. That would suggest the answer is yes in general.
- **Possible obstruction.** The main risk is a group with many subgroups of small index, where there are many chances for $A$ to be biased mod some subgroup. My guess is that this only costs lower-order terms.

**3. Source.** No. I don't recall a specific paper, preprint, forum post or formal proof that settles it.
