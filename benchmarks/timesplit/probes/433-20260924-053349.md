## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I recall, this is resolved, with answer **yes**.
   - Erdős and Graham gave a lower bound of roughly $g(k,n) \geq \frac{n^2}{k-1} - 5n$ and conjectured the asymptotic $g(k,n) \sim \frac{n^2}{k-1}$.
   - I believe Dixmier (1990) proved it. He gave matching upper and lower bounds of approximately this shape, valid for $2 \le k < n$:
     $$\left\lfloor \tfrac{n-2}{k-1}\right\rfloor (n-k+1) - 1 \;\le\; g(k,n) \;\le\; \left(\left\lfloor \tfrac{n-1}{k-1}\right\rfloor - 1\right) n - 1.$$
     These bounds imply the asymptotic.
   - The key idea, as I recall it, has two parts. The lower bound comes from explicit constructions using near-arithmetic-progression sets. The upper bound comes from a combinatorial argument on residues modulo the elements of $A$, of Frobenius-number type.
   - I am not certain of the exact form of these bounds. I am also not sure which regimes of $k$ relative to $n$ they cover cleanly.

2. **Expectation.** Not needed as a guess, since I recall a resolution. If I am wrong about Dixmier, I would still expect "yes", because the Erdős–Graham constructions match the natural upper bound.

3. **Source.** J. Dixmier, "Proof of a conjecture by Erdős and Graham concerning the problem of Frobenius", *Journal of Number Theory* (1990). I recall this from memory and have not verified it.
