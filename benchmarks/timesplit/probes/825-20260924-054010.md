## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I know, the problem is still open. I do not recall any resolution.

   Some background, from memory. Numbers that are sums of distinct proper divisors are called pseudoperfect (or semiperfect). Abundant numbers that are not pseudoperfect are called weird numbers. So the question asks whether weird numbers have bounded abundancy σ(n)/n. It goes back to Benkoski and Erdős (1974). They showed that weird numbers have positive density. They also showed that if w is weird and p is a sufficiently large prime, then wp is weird. However, multiplying by such a prime barely changes σ(n)/n, so that result does not settle the question.

2. **Expected answer (a guess).** I lean weakly towards "no": weird numbers probably exist with arbitrarily large σ(n)/n. This is mostly a heuristic. Weird numbers are plentiful and fairly robust under some constructions, and nothing obvious forces pseudoperfectness once the abundancy is huge. But it is quite possible that very large σ(n)/n forces enough small divisors to make n pseudoperfect, which would give "yes". So I hold this guess with low confidence.

3. **Source.** I do not recall any paper, preprint, forum post or formal proof that settles it. The only source I remember is the original Benkoski–Erdős paper on weird numbers, which poses the question rather than answering it.
