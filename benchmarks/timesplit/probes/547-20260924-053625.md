## Answer

**1. Status.** As far as I know, this is still open in full generality. I am not certain of that.

What I recall:
- The bound $R(T) \le 2n-2$ would follow immediately from the Erdős–Sós conjecture. That conjecture says every graph with average degree greater than $n-2$ contains every tree on $n$ vertices. In a 2-colouring of $K_{2n-2}$, one colour class has more than half the edges, so it has average degree above $n-2$ and would contain $T$.
- Ajtai, Komlós, Simonovits and Szemerédi announced a proof of Erdős–Sós for all sufficiently large $n$. As far as I know, the full proof was never published.
- There are partial results, such as bounds of the form $R(T) \le (2+o(1))n$ and exact results for special classes of trees. Some of these relate to the Burr–Erdős conjecture.

I do not know of a complete published resolution.

**2. Guess.** I expect the answer is **yes**, that is, $R(T) \le 2n-2$ holds. This is a guess. My reasons:
- It follows from the widely believed Erdős–Sós conjecture.
- It is claimed for large $n$ via the AKSS announcement.
- The bound is tight for some trees; for example, it holds with equality for certain stars when $n$ is even.

**3. Source.** I do not recall a specific paper, preprint or formal proof that settles it completely.
