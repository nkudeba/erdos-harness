## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this has been resolved, and in the negative: the components need not be convex. My confidence is moderate, not high.

The question comes from Erdős, Herzog and Piranian, "Metric properties of polynomials" (1958). My recollection is that it was answered negatively soon afterwards, most likely by Ch. Pommerenke around 1959–1961 in his papers on metric properties of complex polynomials. It is possible the counterexample is instead due to A. W. Goodman, who wrote on the convexity of level curves of polynomials in the 1960s. I cannot reliably tell these two apart.

Here is the idea as I understand it:
- When c is very small, each component is a small near-disc around a root, so it is convex.
- The question allows every c up to the first critical value, where two components are about to merge.
- For c near that threshold, a component can develop an inward dent. This happens because |f| is not "radially monotone" around a root once other roots and critical points are at comparable distances.
- One can then pick explicit polynomials, with roots and critical points placed suitably, whose components have a non-convex boundary before any merging occurs.

I do not remember the precise construction.

**2. Guess.** Not needed as a separate item, since I am giving "no" as a recalled answer. If my recollection is wrong, I would still guess "no", because the level sets near the merging threshold look intuitively capable of being dented.

**3. Source.** I have a vague memory of Pommerenke's paper (c. 1959–61) on this problem from Erdős–Herzog–Piranian. I cannot name the exact title or journal with confidence, and I know of no formal proof.
