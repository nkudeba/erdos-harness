## Answer

1. **Status: resolved, as far as I recall. The answer is no.** $S(\pi)=o(n^2)$ fails for every permutation.
   - **Hegyvári (1986)** proved that every $\pi\in S_n$ satisfies $S(\pi)\ge (1/18+o(1))\,n^2$.
   - **Key idea (as I remember it, roughly):** There are about $n^2/2$ consecutive sums in total. A sum can only be repeated a limited number of times, because intervals with equal sums are constrained. Counting the coincidences shows that a positive proportion of the sums must be distinct.
   - **Konieczny (2015)** later showed that for a random permutation, $S(\pi)\sim \frac{1+e^{-2}}{4}\,n^2$ with high probability.
   - I am reasonably but not completely sure of the exact constants and attributions. I am confident about the negative answer and the quadratic lower bound.

2. **Guess:** Not needed, since I believe the problem is resolved. If I were wrong, I would still expect "no".

3. **Sources I recall:**

   - J. Konieczny, "On consecutive sums in permutations" (arXiv, 2015).
