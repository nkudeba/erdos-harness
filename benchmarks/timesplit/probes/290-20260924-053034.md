## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this has been resolved affirmatively: yes, such a $b$ always exists. My recollection is that Wouter van Doorn proved it around 2024, and that erdosproblems.com lists #290 as solved.

   On growth, I vaguely recall linear bounds of roughly the shape $a + c\log a < b(a) \ll a$. As I remember them, the upper bound is something like $b(a) < 4.374a$ for $a > 1$, and the lower bound is something like $b(a) > a + 0.54\log a$. I am not confident in these exact constants.

   On the method, I remember only the rough idea. The denominator shrinks when adding $1/(b+1)$ cancels the top power of some prime $p$ in the reduced denominator of $\sum_{a \le n \le b} 1/n$. One therefore picks $b+1$ to be a suitable multiple of a prime power $p^k$ in a range just above $a$. In that range the multiples of $p^k$ are few, so their contributions can be arranged to cancel at the top $p$-adic level.
