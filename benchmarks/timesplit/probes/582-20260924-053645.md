## Answer

**1. Status: resolved, answer yes.**

This is the edge Folkman problem for $F_e(3,3;4)$. Erdős and Hajnal asked it in 1967. Folkman proved in 1970 that such a graph exists: a $K_4$-free graph in which every 2-colouring of the edges contains a monochromatic triangle. His paper is "Graphs with monochromatic complete subgraphs in every edge coloring," SIAM J. Appl. Math. 18 (1970).

- **Folkman's method.** The construction is explicit and iterative, amalgamation-style, and gives an astronomically large graph.
- **Frankl–Rödl (1986).** They gave a probabilistic proof using random graphs, with a much smaller bound.
- **Spencer (1988).** He brought the bound down to about $3\cdot10^9$ vertices.
- **Lu (2007).** Spectral and eigenvalue arguments on circulant graphs gave 9697 vertices.
- **Dudek–Rödl.** They reduced this to 941, using a reduction to maximum cut and semidefinite programming.
- **Lange–Radziszowski–Xu (2012).** Computer-assisted work on a circulant graph gave 786.

On the other side, the smallest possible order is known to be at least 21 (Bikov–Nenov). I recall these later numerical bounds less reliably than the existence result itself.

**2.** No guess is needed. I remember the resolution.

**3.** The source is Folkman (1970), SIAM J. Appl. Math. Frankl–Rödl and Spencer give later proofs with better bounds.
