## Answer

1. **Status: resolved, as far as I recall.** The answer is **no**: $k(N)$ is not $o(\log N)$.
   - Trivially $k(N) \le \sum_{n\le N} 1/n \approx \log N$.
   - My recollection of the erdosproblems.com page is that Hunter and Sawhney observed that $k(N) = (1-o(1))\log N$. They derived this from Bloom's (2021) strengthening of Croot's results on unit fractions.
   - **Key idea:** Bloom's theorem says that a set of integers in a suitable range with large enough reciprocal sum contains a subset whose reciprocals sum to exactly 1. One then extracts such subsets greedily and disjointly. Each extraction uses up reciprocal sum only slightly more than 1, so you get $(1-o(1))\log N$ disjoint subsets.
   - I am fairly but not fully confident about the attribution and the exact form of the statement.

2. Not applicable as a guess, since I believe it is resolved. If I were guessing, I would still answer "no", because the trivial upper bound looks essentially attainable.

3. The sources I recall are T. Bloom, "On a density conjecture about unit fractions" (2021), together with the Hunter–Sawhney observation recorded on the erdosproblems.com page. I do not recall a separate dedicated paper or a formal proof.
