## Answer

1. **Status.** I do not know of a resolution. As far as I know the problem is open, but I am not confident about that.

   For context, the graph analogue is well studied. Moon–Moser asked about it, and Spencer and others showed a graph on n vertices can have about n − log₂ n − O(log* n) distinct maximal clique sizes, but not n − O(1). Erdős asked whether 3-uniform hypergraphs, which have much more freedom, can close that gap to a bounded loss. I do not recall anyone settling that.

2. **Guess.** I lean towards **no**, but this is only a guess and my confidence is low. My reasoning is loose: in the graph case the loss is forced by a counting or packing argument, and I would expect some weaker analogue to force an unbounded loss for hypergraphs too, perhaps of iterated-logarithm type. It is quite possible that the extra freedom in hypergraphs allows a construction achieving n − O(1). I would not be surprised by either answer.

3. **Source.** No. I do not remember a specific paper, preprint, forum post or formal proof that settles it.
