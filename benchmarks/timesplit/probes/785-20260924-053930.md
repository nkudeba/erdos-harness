## Answer

1. **Status:** I believe the problem is resolved, with the answer **yes**. As I recall it:
   - Danzer showed that infinite additive complements with $A(x)B(x)\sim x$ exist, so the hypothesis is not empty.
   - Sárközy and Szemerédi (around 1994) proved that under this hypothesis $A(x)B(x)-x\to\infty$.
   - Ruzsa later showed this is essentially sharp: for any function $w(x)\to\infty$, there are complements with $A(x)B(x)-x < w(x)$ for large $x$. I am less sure of the exact form of this sharpness result.
   
   I do not remember the details of the Sárközy–Szemerédi proof. My understanding is that it is a combinatorial counting argument showing that $A+B$ must have many repeated representations. I cannot reconstruct it.
