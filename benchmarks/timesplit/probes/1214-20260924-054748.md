## Answer

1. **Resolved, and the answer is yes: $x = y$.**
   - **Who and when:** C. Corrales-Rodrigáñez and R. Schoof settled this in 1997. It is Erdős's "support problem", which he raised around 1988.
   - **Their theorem:** let $x, y$ be nonzero rationals (or elements of a number field). Suppose that for every $n \geq 1$, every prime dividing $x^n - 1$ also divides $y^n - 1$. Then $y$ is a power of $x$.
   - **Applying it both ways:** with equal supports and $x, y \geq 2$, we get $y = x^a$ and $x = y^b$ with $a, b \geq 1$. This forces $a = b = 1$, so $x = y$.
   - **The case $x = 1$:** here $x^n - 1 = 0$ is divisible by every prime. If $y \geq 2$, then $y - 1$ has only finitely many prime divisors, so the supports differ. Hence $y = 1$ as well.
   - **Key idea:** pick primes $\ell$ and consider the Kummer extensions $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^k}, x^{1/\ell^k}, y^{1/\ell^k})$. Chebotarev density then shows that if $y$ were not in the group generated by $x$, there would be primes $p$ for which the order of $x$ mod $p$ is divisible by a higher power of $\ell$ than the order of $y$ mod $p$. Taking $n$ equal to the order of $y$ mod $p$ gives $p \mid y^n - 1$ but $p \nmid x^n - 1$, a contradiction. The same argument applies with $x$ and $y$ swapped.
   - **Caveat:** I have not checked how erdosproblems.com currently lists #1214. The mathematics is settled by the result above, unless the site's version carries some twist I'm not aware of.

2. This is not a guess, but a guess would also be yes.

3. C. Corrales-Rodrigáñez and R. Schoof, "The support problem and its elliptic analogue," *J. Number Theory* 64 (1997), 276–290. I don't recall a formal Lean proof.
