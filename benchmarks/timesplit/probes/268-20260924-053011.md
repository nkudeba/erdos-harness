## Answer

1. **Status.** As far as I recall, the problem has been resolved, and the answer is **yes**: $X$ contains an open set. I attribute this to Vjekoslav Kovač and Terence Tao, in their 2024 arXiv preprint "On several irrationality problems for Ahmes series." My recollection is that they prove the more general statement for any fixed $k$: the set of vectors $\left(\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{n+j}\right)_{0\le j<k}$, over infinite $A$ with $\sum_{n\in A} 1/n<\infty$, contains an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^k$.

   The key idea, as I remember it:
   - They use a general criterion for when the subset sums of a sequence of vectors $v_n \to 0$ fill an open set. Roughly, a greedy or iterative covering argument works if each $v_n$ is controlled by tail sums of later terms, and the vectors are spread out in all directions.
   - Expanding $1/(n+j)$ in powers of $1/n$ shows the vectors $(1/n, 1/(n+1), 1/(n+2))$ behave like $(1/n, 1/n^2, 1/n^3)$ after a linear change of variables.
   - That "moment curve" structure keeps them from degenerating into a hyperplane. Passing to suitable sparse subsequences then gives the required nondegeneracy.
   - One can also force $A$ to be infinite.

   I'm fairly but not completely confident in these details. I'm more sure of the attribution and the direction than of the exact form of the lemma.

3. The source is Kovač–Tao, "On several irrationality problems for Ahmes series," arXiv 2024. I do not recall a formal (Lean) proof.
