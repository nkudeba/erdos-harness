## Answer

**1. Status.** As far as I know, this problem is still open. I do not remember a resolution in either direction.

The relevant background I recall is Erdős and Nathanson's work on partitioning bases into disjoint bases, from around 1979. My memory is that they proved the answer is yes under a stronger hypothesis: if $1_A\ast 1_A(n) > c\log n$ for a suitable constant, roughly $c > 1/\log(4/3)$, then $A$ can be split into two disjoint bases of order $2$. Their proof splits $A$ at random and uses a union bound. The open case is when the representation function tends to infinity only slowly, for example like $\log\log n$. I am not certain of the exact constant or the precise statement.

**2. Expected answer (guess).** My guess is "no". When $1_A\ast 1_A(n)$ grows very slowly, the random-splitting argument breaks down. It seems plausible that someone could build a basis in which some $n$ have only a few representations, arranged so that every 2-colouring of $A$ leaves some $n$ uncovered by one of the two colour classes. This is a weak guess, not a recollection.

**3. Source.** I do not recall any specific paper, preprint, forum post or formal proof that settles the problem.
