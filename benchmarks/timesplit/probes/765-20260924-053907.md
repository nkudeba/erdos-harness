## Answer

1. **Status.** As I understand it, the first-order question is resolved. It is not really a yes/no question; I am reading "yes" as "an asymptotic formula is known." The formula is

   ex(n; C₄) ~ ½ n^{3/2}.

   - **Upper bound (≤ (½ + o(1)) n^{3/2}):** this follows from the Kővári–Sós–Turán counting argument, also due to Reiman. Every pair of vertices has at most one common neighbour. So the sum over vertices v of C(deg v, 2) is at most C(n, 2). Applying convexity then bounds the number of edges.
   - **Lower bound:** Erdős–Rényi–Sós (1966) and Brown (1966) constructed the polarity graph of the projective plane PG(2, q). It has n = q² + q + 1 vertices and about ½ q(q+1)² edges. Density of primes fills in the values of n between these.
   - **Exact values:** Füredi (1983/1996) showed ex(q² + q + 1; C₄) = ½ q(q+1)² for large prime powers q, at least for q even or large.

   There is a finer open question: the second-order term, for example whether ex(n; C₄) = ½ n^{3/2} + ¼ n + O(n^{1/2}). I am not sure whether the erdosproblems.com page for #765 frames the problem as that finer version. If it does, the entry may be marked open.

2. **Guess.** Not applicable for the first-order formula. If the page means the lower-order terms, I would expect those to still be open.

3. **Sources.** Erdős–Rényi–Sós (1966), "On a problem of graph theory"; Brown (1966), "On graphs that do not contain a Thomsen graph"; Füredi, "On the number of edges of quadrilateral-free graphs" (JCTB 1996). I do not recall a formal proof.
