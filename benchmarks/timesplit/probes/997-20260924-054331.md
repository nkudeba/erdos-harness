## Answer

**1. Status.** I don't know of a resolution. I believe the problem is open, but I'm not confident about that. I remember that Erdős claimed a metric version: for almost all α, {αp_n} is not well-distributed. I don't reliably recall a proof that covers every α.

**2. Guess: yes.** I expect {αp_n} is not well-distributed for every α.
- **Rational α:** the fractional parts {αp_n} take only finitely many values. So the sequence is not even uniformly distributed, and the claim is trivial.
- **Irrational α:** well-distribution requires uniform behaviour over every window of k consecutive primes, uniformly in the starting point. Tools like Shiu's strings of congruent consecutive primes, or Maynard–Tao dense clusters, should produce windows of consecutive primes with atypical structure. That in turn should push the fractional parts to cluster in some window.
- **Caveat:** I don't know how to make this work. The primes involved are huge compared with the modulus of a good rational approximation of α, so the obvious argument doesn't close.

**3. Source.** I don't recall any specific paper, preprint, forum post or formal proof that settles it.
