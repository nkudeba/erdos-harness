## Answer

**1. Status.** I believe this has been resolved, and the answer is **no**. I recall this with moderate confidence, not certainty.

- **Hindman, Leader and Strauss**, "Pairwise sums in colourings of the reals" (Abh. Math. Semin. Univ. Hambg., c. 2017). They showed, assuming CH, that there is a finite colouring of ℝ in which no uncountable set has monochromatic pairwise sums. They also showed that if the colour classes are measurable or have the Baire property, large monochromatic sumset structures do exist.
- **Péter Komjáth**, "A certain 2-coloring of the reals" (Real Analysis Exchange, c. 2016). I believe this gives a 2-colouring of ℝ, in ZFC, with no uncountable A whose pairwise sums a+b (a≠b) are all one colour. That settles Erdős's question negatively.
- **Key idea, as I recall it:** view ℝ as a vector space over ℚ with a Hamel basis. Colour each real by a combinatorial property of its coordinates, such as the parity of something defined through a well-ordering of the basis. This is a Sierpiński-style construction. For any uncountable A, you can then find pairs whose sums get both colours.
- **Related work:** Komjáth, Leader, Russell, Shelah, D. Soukup and Vidnyánszky, "Infinite monochromatic sumsets for colourings of the reals" (Proc. AMS, 2019), studied the countably infinite version.

I am less sure of two details: exactly which paper handles 2 colours in ZFC, and whether the erdosproblems.com page credits Komjáth or Hindman–Leader–Strauss.

**2. Expected answer.** Not applicable as a pure guess, since I recall a resolution. The answer I recall is no.

**3. Specific source.** Komjáth, "A certain 2-coloring of the reals," Real Analysis Exchange (around 2016). Also Hindman–Leader–Strauss (2017). I am confident about the direction of the result but less sure of the exact attribution.
