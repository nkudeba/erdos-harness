## Answer

1. **Status: I believe this is resolved, with answer yes.** This is the planar (complex) case of the Littlewood–Offord problem.
   - Erdős proved the real case using Sperner's theorem.
   - The complex case was proved by Katona (1966) and, independently, by Kleitman (1965).
   - Kleitman (1970) extended it to vectors in any dimension, and to Hilbert/normed settings, for open balls of radius 1. Equivalently, these are sets of diameter less than 2.
   - The key idea is Kleitman's inductive argument. It partitions the sign vectors into chain-like families, in the spirit of symmetric chain decompositions. Within each family, two sums that differ in a single sign change are at least 2 apart, because $2|z_i| \geq 2$. So each family meets an open unit disc in at most one point, and counting the families gives the bound $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$.
   - One caveat: the disc should be taken open. For a closed disc, boundary cases need care.

2. Not a guess: my answer of yes comes from recalling the theorems above.

3. Sources I recall:

   - G. Katona, "On a conjecture of Erdős and a stronger form of Sperner's theorem," Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. (1966).
   - D. Kleitman, "On a lemma of Littlewood and Offord on the distributions of linear combinations of vectors," Adv. Math. 5 (1970).
