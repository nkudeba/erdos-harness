## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this problem is resolved, in the affirmative (the answer is yes). My recollection is that Erdős, Sárközy and Sós proved it in a paper from around the late 1980s or 1990s. I think they showed something stronger: almost all even numbers up to about N can be written as a monochromatic sum, specifically at least N/2 − O(N^{1−1/2^{2k+2}}) of them, possibly with a slightly different exponent. That count is at least cN with c close to 1/2. I also vaguely recall a later improvement of the error term, perhaps by Hegyvári. I cannot reconstruct the key idea with any confidence. I believe it is a density/pigeonhole argument showing that each large colour class has a sumset covering most even numbers. I may be misattributing details, including the exact exponent and year.

2. **Expected answer.** If my memory is wrong, my guess would still be yes. A positive-density colour class A has a sumset A+A (with distinct summands) of linear size. The difficulty is only in handling many sparse classes, and Schur-type arguments suggest monochromatic sums are plentiful.

3. **Source.** My tentative source is Erdős, Sárközy and Sós, on monochromatic sums in colourings of {1,…,N}. I cannot give the exact title or year reliably. I do not recall a formal proof.
