## Answer

1. **Status.** I believe this is resolved, in the affirmative (**yes**). As I recall, Szemerédi proved it in 1976, in "On a problem of P. Erdős", Journal of Number Theory. He showed that if all products $ab$ are distinct, then $|A||B| \ll N^2/\log N$. Erdős had earlier obtained weaker bounds and posed the question. The bound is sharp up to the constant. One example: take $A$ to be the primes up to $N$, which gives $|A| \approx N/\log N$, and $B$ to be something like $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ restricted suitably. I am less sure of the exact extremal example.

   On the key idea, my recollection is vague. I think it is a combinatorial counting argument: factor each element as a "smooth part" times large prime factors. Then use the distinct-products condition, which forbids certain rectangle configurations, to bound the number of pairs. I do not recall the details reliably.

2. Not applicable, since I believe it is resolved. If I am wrong about the resolution, I would still expect **yes**.

3. Szemerédi, E., "On a problem of P. Erdős", J. Number Theory 8 (1976). I also recall that erdosproblems.com lists #490 as proved via this reference. I am not certain of the exact citation details.
