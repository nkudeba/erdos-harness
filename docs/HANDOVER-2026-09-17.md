## Step 1 (do this first, ~$12): finish masked #275

**The question this answers.** Each cycle the residual gets simpler by the metric, but nothing guarantees the
descent converges: a hypothesis can always be split into two syntactically simpler ones whose conjunction is
exactly as hard, and the metric cannot tell that from real progress. #275 is a controlled test, because a
solved problem's descent *must* bottom out in provable facts.

- If both lemmas discharge, the loop went from a de-identified statement to a complete proof by derivation
  alone. That is the claim the whole design rests on and it will have been demonstrated once.
- If they split into four more, the loop can shrink a residual indefinitely without approaching a proof.
  Then do step 2 before anything else.

```bash
python3 harness.py run \
  --problem benchmarks/deductive/problems/erdos_80317.json \
  --resume runs/20260917-020550-707932-bench-erdos80317 \
  --mode research --allow-research --benchmark --skip-literature --no-brief --masked \
  --research-loop deductive --run-budget 40 --research-cycles 1 --hyps-per-cycle 2 \
  --min-child-budget 4 --refute off --backend claude-cli --model claude-opus-5-5
```

`--run-budget` is **cumulative over the run**, which has already spent $27.46, so 40 allows ~$12 more.
`--refute off` because both lemmas were already cleared by the refutation stage in the last cycle; re-running
it costs ~$3 and would learn nothing. One lane at a time: check `pgrep -f "harness.py run"` first. Watch with
`tail -1 runs/20260917-020550-707932-bench-erdos80317/SUMMARY.line`, judge by `RESIDUAL.md`, never by
`lemmas=`. Record the outcome as a row in `benchmarks/deductive/RESULTS.md` (benchmark outcomes on solved
problems never go in the Atlas).

## Step 2 (only if step 1 splits again): a progress rule, not a better metric

A cycle counts as progress only if a hypothesis was **discharged**, not merely split. Two consecutive cycles
that only split mean the descent has stalled: the loop should record the obstruction and stop rather than
keep refining. Implement in `_run_program_deductive` next to the existing `residual_improves` check, with a
test in `tests/test_deductive_mode.py`.

## Step 4: before any open target, attack both directions

The deductive loop only ever builds toward proving the target. On an open problem the target may be false,
and a budget can be spent deriving conditions on counterexamples to something that has one. `--open` already
formalises and attacks a statement and its negation; the deductive loop does not yet do this. Decide whether
`--open` composes with `--research-loop deductive` or whether the loop needs its own two-direction form, and
test it on a solved problem with a **negative** answer (#649 or #16, both in the benchmark set).
