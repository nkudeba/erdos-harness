  A partial proof claimed by Rajveer Kapoor (using Claude Opus 5 (xhigh effort)), 2026-08-21: Write $n=|A|$, $\kappa=\operatorname{diam}(A)/n^2$, $t=|A+A|$, $Q(A)=t^{-1}\sum(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2$. Theorem. There is $c>0$ with $Q(A)\ge c\min\{\log\frac1{\kappa-1},\log n\}$ for all large $n$. Proof by scale decomposition: cut the interval dyadically, apply Cauchy-Schwarz per window, and use the sharp energy inequality $\sum_k m_k^2/\ell_k\le1+o(1)$ to force the densities down. Summing scales gives the logarithm. So the answer is yes for every family with $\operatorname{diam}(A_n)=(1+o(1))n^2$ (Singer, Bose-Chowla, Ruzsa, affine images), and for every dyadically non-concentrated family. Also $\liminf f(n)\ge16$, improved to $\approx16.13$. Such a family needs $\kappa\in[1+e^{-O(C)},\sqrt C/4]$ and $\alpha>1/2$. Not claimed: the general case.
