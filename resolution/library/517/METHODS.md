#1154 (analysis; not disprovable): Does there exist, for every $\alpha \in [0,1]$, a ring or field in $\mathbb{R}$ with Hausdorff dimension $\alpha$?
  A partial proof claimed by Yi Wang (using gpt5.6sol), 2026-08-28: Let $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be a subring such that the set $I := \{X \in 2^\omega : \beta(X) \in E\}$, $\beta(X) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty X(n) 2^{-(n+1)}$, is closed under the Turing join $X \oplus Y$. $\beta$ and $\beta \times \beta$ preserve Hausdorff dimension. The join map $J(X,Y) = X \oplus Y$ gives a bi-Lipschitz equivalence between $(I \times I, \rho_2)$ and $(I, \rho)$; since squaring the metric halves the dimension. Let $d := \dim_H(E)$, $0<d<1$, we have $\dim_H(E \times E) = 2d$. For each $a,b \in E$, let $\ell_{a,b} = \{(x,y) : y = ax + b\}$. The family $L = \{\ell_{a,b} : a,b \in E\}$ has dimension $2d$, and each line intersects $E \times E$ in an affine copy of $E$, hence has dimension $d$. By the weak furstenburg set conjecture (arXiv:2106.03338 thm1.1), $\dim_H(E \times E) \ge 2d + \varepsilon(d,2d) > 2d$, a contradiction. Thus $d \in \{0,1\}$.
