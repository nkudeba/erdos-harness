## Page
Let $A\subset\mathbb{N}$ be an additive basis of order $k$ which is minimal, in the sense that if $B\subset A$ is any infinite set then $A\backslash B$ is not a basis of order $k$. 
Must there exist an infinite $B\subset A$ such that $A\backslash B$ is a basis of order $k+1$?

 Let \(A\subset\mathbb N\) be an asymptotic additive basis of order \(k\), and suppose that \(A\) is strongly minimal under infinite deletions in the sense that, for every infinite \(B\subset A\), the set \(A\setminus B\) is not an asymptotic basis of order \(k\). Must there exist an infinite \(B\subset A\) such that \(A\setminus B\) is an asymptotic basis of order \(k+1\)?
We give a complete answer. For \(k=1\), the answer is yes. For every \(k\ge2\), the answer is no. More strongly, for every \(k\ge2\), we construct a set \(A\subset\mathbb N\) such that \(A\) is an asymptotic basis of order \(k\), is ordinarily minimal at order \(k\), is strongly minimal under infinite deletions at order \(k\), and satisfies the stronger property that \(A\setminus B\) is not an asymptotic basis of order \(k+1\) for every infinite
\(B\subset A\). The examples may be chosen thin, with\[
A(x) = \lvert A \cap [0, x] \rvert = O_k(x^{1/k}) 
\]which is optimal up to constants for bases of order \(k\).
