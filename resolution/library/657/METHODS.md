#982 (geometry, convex, distances; falsifiable): If $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ form a convex polygon then some vertex has at least $\lfloor \frac{n}{2}\rfloor$ different distances to other vertices.
  A partial proof claimed by Scott Duke Kominers (using GPT 5.6 Sol, Claude Fable 5), 2026-07-25: Recording a (very slight) strengthening of the lower bound: Writing $f(n)$ for the target quantity as on the problem page, we have $$f(n) \geq \left(\frac{13}{36} + \frac{3}{5270}\right)n - O(1),$$ improving the additive term in the [NPPZ13] coefficient by a factor of roughly $12.92$. This is still, of course, quite a long way from the conjectured $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$. The argument refines the good-edge--and--witness iteration used by [NPPZ13]: the main new ingredients are an exact three-neighborhood matching for the support sets of successive smallest enclosing circles, anchored at the original support set (so the baseline good-edge count inside the original caps needs no deletion correction); adaptive accounting that credits the exact quadratic imbalance of the original cap sizes in every case; and an aggregate escape test exploiting the drift of all three original support points simultaneously. Comments welcome and highly appreciated!
  Notes: Any improvement of the constant $\frac{7}{8}$ in Lemma 12 of [NPPZ13] toward the $\frac{3}{5}$ barrier of Lev--Pinchasi would immediately improve the bound further; and no bound beyond $\frac{5}{12}n$ can follow from the isosceles-triangle reduction alone.
