## Page
Can $\mathbb{N}$ be partitioned into two sets, each of which can be permuted to avoid monotone 3-term arithmetic progressions?

 Partial result — not a claimed solution of this problem, which remains open. The canonical dyadic-blocks partition cannot witness a YES answer. Let $S_A = \bigcup_{k \text{ even}} (2^{k-1}, 2^k]$ and $S_B = \mathbb{Z}^+ \setminus S_A$ — the natural first candidate, since the completion $2y-x$ of a within-block pair lands in the other part. We prove: $S_A$ admits no permutation of order type $\omega$ avoiding monotone 3-term APs; hence any partition witnessing a YES must differ from the dyadic one.
Proof shape: (1) an exact reformulation of permutability via stage functions into finite chunks (in these coordinates the Davis–Entringer–Graham–Simmons theorem, in its monotone form, has a four-line proof); (2) an unconditional reduction: if an "order gadget" on $(M, 2M]$ — AP-freeness plus fifteen precedence axioms generated by the values 15 and 16 — is infeasible at infinitely many dyadic scales $M = 2^{2t-1}$, then $S_A$ is not permutable; (3) a hand proof that already a three-axiom core, $\{2M-5 \prec M+5,\ 2M-3 \prec M+6,\ 2M-10 \prec M+3\}$, is inconsistent with AP-freeness for every $M \equiv 0 \pmod 8$ (sharp: satisfiable at every other residue; the residue enters at exactly one point, a class condition on two "flood centres" near $3M/2$).
Discovery was heavily machine-assisted (SAT solvers), but the final chain is human-readable; its finitely many schema instances are machine-audited by three independently written checkers (mutation-tested), with DRAT certificates for the finite base cases. Independent verification is invited: a one-command reproduction kit re-runs the complete stack in ~17 minutes on a laptop. Paper (PDF), code, and certificates: github.com/Wkasel/erdos197 (release v1.0). An arXiv submission is in progress (endorsement pending); the ID will follow in a reply.
Notably the theorem is consistent with Geneson's arXiv:2608.12604: his $\alpha_{\mathbb{N}}(3) \ge 2/3$ witness is built from exactly these octave blocks minus their bottom slivers — precisely the region where our obstruction lives. Together the two results locate the obstruction at the block bottoms sharply.
