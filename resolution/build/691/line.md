=== GOAL

**About the literal Lean statement.** It is discharged by `TSMain.answer := fun A ↦ (multiples A).HasDensity 1` followed by `rfl`. That is the tautology barrier. Under the convention for `answer`-style statements this should not count, and I count it as 0 above; the harness should confirm this explicitly. A genuine settlement needs an intrinsic criterion valid for all A. The claimed T4 says "all covers diverge" fails once Ω is unbounded, and no log-weighted replacement is in sight. So no settling line is plausible within days. I am choosing the line with the best chance of major progress.

**Target theorem (bounded-rank cover criterion, "T3" made rigorous).** Fix r ≥ 1. Let A ⊆ ℕ≥2 with Ω(a) ≤ r for all a ∈ A. Call G ⊆ ℕ≥2 a cover of A if every a ∈ A has a divisor g ∈ G. Then:

  M_A has natural density 1 ⇔ every cover G of A has ∑_{g∈G} 1/g = ∞.

The proof should have explicit constants: if every cover of A∩[1,N] costs ≥ M, then d(M_{A∩[1,N]}) ≥ 1 − ε(M, r), with ε → 0 as M → ∞ for fixed r. This would be the first exact, intrinsic characterization of Behrend sequences for a natural infinite class beyond pairwise-coprime sets, with explicit constants. Its proof would be the first transfer of Park–Pham to multiplicative number theory. Expert significance: 2–3, and 3 if paired with the quantitative loss (K log r)^{r} showing exactly where unbounded Ω breaks it. It does not settle `erdos_691`.

=== WHY THIS LINE
- **The easy direction is already solid from KNOWN results.** Take a cover G with ∑1/g < ∞. Then M_A ⊆ M_G, and Heilbronn–Rohrbach on the truncations plus Davenport–Erdős (lower density = lim d(M_{A∩[1,N]})) give 1 − δ(M_G) ≥ ∏(1 − 1/g) > 0. Hence the density is not 1.
- **The hard direction is a "probability-1 Kahn–Kalai" statement.** It is stopped in general only by the log ℓ loss, as in the Kahn–Kalai barrier, and T4 says that loss accumulates. With Ω ≤ r, ℓ ≤ r, so the loss is a fixed constant (K log r)^r. An infinite cover cost then absorbs it.
- **It respects the other barriers.**
  - It is non-additive, consistent with T2.
  - "Exists a finite-cost cover" is the F_σ/Σ-side description of non-Behrend, consistent with the descriptive-complexity barrier.
  - It does not rely on Tenenbaum/Ford regularity.
- **Caveat.** The team has NO validated results yet. T2–T7 are labels, not referee-checked claims, so nothing below may cite them as established.

3. **(Encoding; standard, needs a write-up)** Model Ẑ by independent Bernoulli variables:
   - The ground set is X = {(p, j)}, where (p, j) is present with probability 1/p.
   - Set v_p = the length of the initial run of successes. This gives P(v_p ≥ j) = p^{-j}.
   - Then a ∣ n iff S_a ⊆ ω, where S_a = {(p, j) : j ≤ v_p(a)} and |S_a| = Ω(a) ≤ r.
   - So M_A is the up-set generated by rank-≤ r sets, and the cost of g equals 1/g.
4. **(Easy direction)** Write the proof from step "easy direction" above in full. KNOWN results suffice.
5. **(Compactness)** Show the following: if every cover of A has infinite cost, then C_N := min cover cost of A∩[1,N] tends to ∞.
   - Candidate cover elements are divisors of elements of A, a countable set.
   - Take a diagonal subsequence of indicator vectors.
   - Each a is covered by one of its finitely many divisors in all later covers, so it is covered in the limit.
   - Fatou bounds the cost of the limit cover.
6. **(Weighted Park–Pham)** Obtain the version for product measures with heterogeneous weights q_x scaled by a common factor s.
   - The statement needed: if every cover at scale s costs ≥ 1/2 and 1 ≥ K s log(r/ε), then P(up-set) ≥ 1 − ε.
   - Either adapt the Park–Pham (JAMS 2024) proof directly, or reduce via OR-copies, which preserves rank r.
   - With OR-copies, prove that a cheap cover of the copy family pulls back to a cheap cover of the original, with constant loss.
   - Check the ε-dependence of Park–Pham and FKNP before relying on it. Do not invent the statement.
7. **(Scaling)** Since every |S| ≤ r, cost at scale t ≥ t^r · cost at scale 1. If C_N ≥ M, take t = (2M)^{-1/r}; every cover at scale t then costs ≥ 1/2. Step 6 gives d(M_{A∩[1,N]}) ≥ 1 − ε once M ≥ (K log(r/ε))^r / 2. Let N → ∞ and use Davenport–Erdős: δ = 1, hence natural density 1.
8. **(Extension / significance)** Run the same argument with Ω(a) ≤ r(a) growing. Find the largest growth, e.g. r ≤ c·log log log a or a cost condition weighted by (K log Ω(g))^{Ω(g)}, for which the losses stay summable. This records exactly how far the cover criterion extends, matching the T7 gap claim.
9. **(Optional Lean)** Formalize the easy direction and the r = 1 case (prime powers; independence plus Borel–Cantelli) as checked lemmas.

=== PITFALLS
- **Do not present `rfl` or any density reformulation as a solution.** This includes Davenport–Erdős limits and Haar-measure statements; they fall under the tautology barrier.
- **Do not cite T2–T7 as validated.** No referee has checked any team output.
- **Valuation events are nested, not independent.** Use the (p, j) encoding. Treating "p^j ∣ n" for different j as independent is wrong.
- **Park–Pham is stated for uniform p and finite families, with the threshold at probability 1/2.** The probability-1 regime needs:
  - the ε-version,
  - the heterogeneous-weight transfer (step 6), and
  - the truncation/compactness step (step 5).

  Each of these is a separate obligation, and skipping any one is the likely referee failure.
- **Lean conventions.**
  - `multiples` excludes 0. An element 0 ∈ A contributes nothing.
  - 1 ∈ A makes A trivially Behrend; the theorem assumes A ⊆ ℕ≥2.
  - Check which `Set.HasDensity` is meant (Mathlib vs formal-conjectures, and the ambient-set argument) before formalizing.
- **Do not extend the criterion to unbounded Ω.** The claimed T4 counterexample (perfect-matching hypergraphs on prime blocks) shows "all covers diverge" ⇏ Behrend there. Any proof whose loss is not bounded in r has a bug.
- **First-moment trap.** Covers whose elements are all of A itself reduce to ∑1/a. The block sequence with η_k = 1/k shows this cannot be the criterion. Quantify over all covers.
