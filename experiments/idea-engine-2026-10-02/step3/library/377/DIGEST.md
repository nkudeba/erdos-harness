 The latter claim looks doable with current technology. It suffices to show that, for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and any $1 \leq k \leq \log^{1-\varepsilon} n$, that $\sum_{n^{1/(k+1)} < p \leq n^{1/k}} 1_{p\not \mid \binom{2n}{n}} \frac{1}{p} \ll \frac{\varepsilon}{k}$. A necessary condition for $p\not \mid \binom{2n}{n}$ is that $\{ \frac{n}{p^j} \} \leq \frac{1}{2}$ for all $j$. I think just taking $j = k-1, k-2, \dots, k-J$ for some $J \sim \log \frac{1}{\varepsilon}$, combined with known prime number theorems in short intervals, should be able to get the required bound after some lengthy but routine calculations.
The main problem looks hard though, because of the contribution of the very small primes, where the difficulties are similar to those in #376.
