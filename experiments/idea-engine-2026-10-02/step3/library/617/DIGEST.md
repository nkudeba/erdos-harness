 The r=3 case of this conjecture was proved 21 years before [ErGy99]:
K. M. Chung and C. L. Liu, "A generalization of Ramsey theory for graphs", Discrete Mathematics 21 (1978), 117–127, define (for c=3 colours, d=2) the quantity $R^3_2(K_i, K_j, K_l)$ — the least $p$ such that every 3-colouring of $E(K_p)$ contains an $\alpha$–$\beta$ $K_i$, an $\alpha$–$\gamma$ $K_j$, or a $\beta$–$\gamma$ $K_l$ (colours $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$) — and their Theorem 3.6 proves $R^3_2(K_4,K_4,K_4)=10$, with a complete hand proof. 
In this diagonal case a $K_4$ on any 2-subset of the colours is exactly a $K_4$ with a colour missing, so this is the $r=3$ case of the present problem. Their Theorem 2.6 also gives the matching lower bound via a product colouring ($R^3_2(K_i,K_j,K_l) > (i-1)(j-1)$, which is $>9$ here).
The general-$r$ statement is likewise a named quantity in several later literatures that appear disjoint from [ErGy99]'s citation graph: as a "weakened Ramsey number" (in Chung–Liu-style notation, $R^r_{r-1}(K_{r+1})$; H. Harborth and M. Möller, "Weakened Ramsey numbers", Discrete Applied Mathematics 95 (1999), 279–284, record the Chung–Liu value in their notation as $R_{2,3}(K_4)=10$), and as a diagonal "complementary Ramsey number" (A. Munemasa and M. Shinohara, arXiv:1406.2050; J. Indones. Math. Soc. 25 (2019), 146–153). 
In particular, the present problem for $r$ is equivalent to $R^r_{r-1}(K_{r+1}) \le r^2+1$ (with equality whenever the affine plane of order $r$ exists, e.g., $r=5$), and the case $r=5$ appears to be open in all of these communities.
