## What the problem asks

A "covering system" is a finite list of rules like "numbers that leave remainder 3 when divided by 8". Each rule has a divisor, called its modulus, and a remainder. The list covers if every whole number, positive or negative, obeys at least one rule. The question asks whether such a list exists when every modulus is one less than a prime number of at least 5 — so the allowed moduli are 4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 18, and so on. The moduli must all be different; reusing a modulus with a new remainder is not allowed. This problem is open on the published catalogue. Nobody knows the answer, yes or no.

## What we found

The most useful thing checked in this run is a parity split. Every allowed modulus is even, because a prime of at least 5 is odd. So each rule catches only even numbers or only odd numbers. Halving everything, the problem becomes: find **two** covering systems at once, with no modulus shared between them, drawn from the same thin pool of allowed halved moduli. That is much harder than finding one. A second checked fact: no allowed modulus leaves remainder 2 when divided by 6, which deletes 8, 14, 20, 26 and more. A third: any solution needs at least 18 rules, and a prime of at least 71.
