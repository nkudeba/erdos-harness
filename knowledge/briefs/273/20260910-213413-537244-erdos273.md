## What the problem asks

A "congruence" is a rule like "every number that leaves remainder 3 when divided by 7". The number you divide by is the "modulus". A "covering system" is a finite set of such rules, with all moduli different, that together catch every whole number — no number escapes. The question asks whether you can build one using only moduli of a special shape: each modulus must be one less than a prime number that is at least 5. So allowed moduli are 4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 18, and so on. Nobody knows the answer. The problem is listed as open, and this run did not settle it.

## What we found

The most useful thing is a checked structural fact. Every allowed modulus is one less than an odd prime, so it is even. Because of that, each rule lands entirely inside the even numbers or entirely inside the odd numbers. Halving everything splits the question in two: a solution is exactly a pair of covering systems, built from halved moduli, that share no modulus at all. Two further checked facts: no allowed modulus leaves remainder 2 on division by 6, and any solution needs at least 18 rules. ## What would move it

Two honest routes. First, exhibit an example: two covering systems whose halved moduli are all different and never shared between the two. That would be a finite object anyone could check. Searching below least common multiple 55,440 is pointless, since the screen already excludes it; the real work starts just above. Second, prove no such pair can exist, by showing the available moduli simply cannot supply enough coverage twice over. The difficulty is concrete: the small, efficient moduli can only be used by one of the two halves. Any future search should encode the halved two-system form, skip moduli that leave remainder 2 on division by 6, and reject candidates with fewer than 18 rules.
